Dejan Kulusevski aims pointed criticism at Tottenham over 526-day injury lay-off
Dejan Kulusevski missed the entirety of the 2025/26 season with a knee injury which required surgery and has now hinted at friction behind the scenes with Tottenham
Add to preferred sources

Felix Keith
 Senior Sports Reporter
12:19, 11 Nov 2026
View 3 Images
Dejan Kulusevski was out injured for 526 days
(Image: Carl Recine/Getty Images)
Dejan Kulusevski has offered a cryptic piece of criticism over the way Tottenham handled his injury, saying he was &quot;disappointed in people&quot; during the ordeal.
‌
Kulusevski went 526 days without making an appearance for Spurs due to a serious knee injury. He missed the whole 2025/26 season before making an emotional comeback in the 
3-1 win over Coventry City
 last month.
Speaking after the match,
 the Sweden international said the process had seemed &quot;impossible&quot; at times and he feared he might never play again. Having initially been ruled out with a &#x27;knock&#x27;, Kulusevski&#x27;s prolonged absence was shrouded in mystery for Spurs fans.
Tottenham overhauled their medical department over the summer after missing Kulusevski, 
James Maddison
, Wilson Odobert and Xavi Simmons to serious knee injuries.

Having returned to the Sweden squad for the first time in two years, Kulusevski opened up to Swedish reporters. &quot;I have been disappointed in people during this period but at the same time I have to forgive everything,&quot; the midfielder said.
&quot;It is also written in the Bible. That is how I have moved on. It is difficult for all people. Unfortunately, we look after our own interests first, often. It has been a tough period because sometimes you wish you had received more help and such.
&quot;But at the same time I understand that we are all part of a &#x27;business&#x27;. I have moved on and I love being at the club. I received a very good welcome when I came back.&quot;
Tottenham declined to comment on the subject. Kulusevski&#x27;s injury was sustained in a seemingly innocuous challenge with Marc Guehi which led to a lengthy recovery process, which remarkably involved the offer of a knee transplant.

&quot;The answer to why it took so long is that there are people who have never even come back from injuries like this,&quot; he added. &quot;Above all, we should be happy that it was possible to come back.
&quot;There are many who have stopped playing their sport due to cartilage damage. Why it took so long is that we don&#x27;t know more.
&quot;We did what we thought was best and it didn&#x27;t work. After that we had to try another operation. Then many people were nervous when they realised that it didn&#x27;t seem to work either.
&quot;I had to sit all summer and talk to other doctors about having another operation. It would be a knee transplant. That I had replaced my knee with someone else&#x27;s.

&quot;Then I said no: &#x27;We&#x27;ll do it like this, if I have pain when I play, I have to play with pain&#x27;. I trained and tried to live like a normal person. I trusted that it would work out. So far I&#x27;ve been right.&quot;
Sweden manager Graham Potter will use Kulusevski carefully with the 26-year-old only having made substitute appearances for his club this season.
&quot;We are of course in contact with Tottenham, and it is logical that we work closely with them,” he said. &quot;They have worked with Dejan for so long to get him back to the level he is at now.
&quot;Therefore, it is reasonable that we have a close dialogue. We have a pretty good idea of how we can use him. But the most important thing for us is that he is here.&quot;
