We battled back on Wednesday night to play out a 2-2 draw in the Champions League against Bayern Munich at the Allianz Arena.
The Gunners led in the first half through Ebere Eze, who replaced Bukayo Saka early on after our No. 7 sustained a quad injury, though Alphonso Davies brought Bayern level before the break.
Harry Kane gave the home side the lead from the penalty spot after the interval, but Declan Rice slotted home a fine finish with eight minutes remaining to keep us unbeaten in the league phase.
Mikel Arteta addressed the media in Bavaria following the final whistle, and you can read his press conference below.
On his thoughts on the result…
We’ll take the draw, but it doesn’t mean we’re really happy about it because we prepared a team to win the game - you could see that from right at the beginning, with the mindset, the purpose the adventure team had to be very dominant and cause Bayern problems. We pressed them, attacked them, and we were the better team for 20, 25 minutes and scored the goal. Unfortunately we had the injury to Bukayo which was a big setback, but Ebs jumps in and the team remains in the same way. But the margins are so small - we get something wrong after the corner, and in two passes, they’re in front of goal. In another one, it was the shape, and Harry Kane can put the ball 30 metres along the floor between us for Alphonso Davies, and that’s the level. After that, it was two teams that wanted to press each other and attack, constantly very alive to hurt the opponent. When we went down, we showed an incredible composure and spirit to keep going and attack them, the finishers came in and made an impact as well. After we scored, you saw the reaction of the team to take the ball, wanting to attack them and go again to win the game. I think that tells you a lot about the team, especially looking at Bayern over the last two or three years with the teams that have come here, and the manner they have been battered. When you look at their team and their coach, that’s when I have to give credit to our team and our players.
On Bukayo’s injury…
It’s his quad. It was early in the game, and the level of intensity his game requires is something incredible. The quality, strength and attention you need has to be at the highest level, and those things happen unfortunately. It doesn’t look good at all, but we have to wait and see.
On his main takeaways from the game…
The personality and the courage we had, to get the ball off them and attack them, to be very composed, and especially to change gear from really intense when everything is man-to-man, to after the game, when the heart rate is 180. It’s a different game to what we usually face in the Premier League.
On the character we showed…
The character, personality and quality we showed to understand what the game required, because the game changed and they pressed in two different ways, what the team understood and executed was really, really good. Execution-wise, we had some great moments - we could have been even better and more clinical in certain aspects with our final shot - but overall, it’s a game we can take a lot of positives from.
On Bukayo’s injury giving other attacking players opportunities…
I’m really impressed with all our forwards. I thought Ebs had a great game, not just the goal but his actions, overall performance and without the ball as well. When the others came in, I thought they really contributed to both the energy and providing the tools needed for the team to get something out of the game. So, I’m really pleased again.
On whether he was surprised to see Alphonso Davies play a more attacking role…
We expected Luis Diaz to start, but we were very aware it was going to be a long game, so we understood and thought about that, to have two different approaches as well. You cannot sustain that intensity for 95 minutes - you saw that yesterday with PSG and Barcelona. But every player that they have is super-quality.
