  3. Sky Blues fail to build on successive victoriespublished at 23:15 BST
    23:15 BST

    Michael Emons
    BBC Sport journalist

    Frank Lampard's Coventry City came into the match against Tottenham in confident mood after they had defeated Nottingham Forest and played superbly to beat Newcastle 4-1 at home a week ago.

    Brandon Thomas-Asante got into a good position inside the opening 20 seconds, but his dangerous cross was cut out by home goalkeeper Antonin Kinsky.

    Tatsuhiro Sakamoto had an effort deflected over by Jan Paul van Hecke and only a superb sliding challenge from Micky van de Ven denied Thomas-Asante a shooting chance.

    Jack Rudoni, who also scored in the win over Newcastle, got a goal back but that proved to be Coventry's only shot on target.

    After quickly conceding again, the visitors had plenty of possession, but Lampard will be disappointed they could not test Kinsky again and never really looked like scoring an equaliser.

    Coventry drop to 17th, and have two vital home games this month against sides below them - Fulham on Saturday and Sunderland seven days later.

  9. Tottenham v Coventry City: Key stats and talking pointspublished at 13:41 BST 18 October
    13:41 BST 18 October

    Paul Birch
    BBC Sport journalist

    Tottenham go in search of a first league win of the season against a Coventry City side who have roared into form with back-to-back victories. BBC Sport takes a look at some of the key themes going into Monday's fixture.

    Can Tottenham banish home woes to record a first victory?

    In the wake of their draw with Manchester United, Tottenham headed to Marbella for a few days of warm weather training and, more importantly, squad bonding. "The target is to build the team," head coach Roberto de Zerbi explained. "We are a great group of players, but we are not a team yet."

    His side certainly looked like more of a cohesive unit at Old Trafford, but their slick approach play was squandered by yet more profligacy in front of goal as their winless start stretched to six league matches.

    Only two top-flight sides have completed more passes in the final third than Spurs' 770 and just three teams have had more touches in the opposition box than their 182. Yet a shot conversion rate of 3.6% is the worst in the league and De Zerbi's side have underperformed their xG by 3.6 goals.

    Three goals after six league matches is the second lowest tally in their history, beaten only by the two scored at the start of 2006-07.

    They are still yet to lead in a league match this season or score a goal before the 86th minute.

    One thing they still cannot count on is home comforts. They have managed a paltry five wins in their past 36 league matches at Tottenham Hotspur Stadium, dating back to November 2024.

    Moreover, they have fallen behind in a remarkable 52 of their 79 home league games since the start of 2022-23.

    Sky-high Sky Blues looking to make it a hat-trick of wins

    Coventry travel to north London on the crest of a wave having made it back-to-back wins in style with a 4-1 thrashing of Newcastle. Six points from six games is the same as Manchester United have mustered and is a long way from the zero points and zero goals they had taken from their opening four matches.

    These crucial - and potentially season altering - victories over Nottingham Forest and Newcastle mean the Sky Blues have the chance to become only the fourth top-flight side to lose their opening four matches to a season then win three in a row after Derby County in 1899-00, Arsenal in 1923-24 and Aston Villa in 1997-98.

    A victory would also mark the first time they have won three Premier League matches in succession since a run of five between January and February 1998 under Gordon Strachan.

    Although their most famous win of all came against Tottenham in the 1987 FA Cup final, their past five meetings have all ended in defeat and they last won away at Spurs in 1997.

    However, that victory was particularly memorable as their 2-1 win on the final day of the season helped secure their top-flight status in highly dramatic circumstances.

    In 19th position at kick off, goals from Dion Dublin and Paul Williams helped them leapfrog both Sunderland and Middlesbrough to finish one place and one point above the drop zone.

    One man who does knows how to avoid defeat against Tottenham is head coach Frank Lampard.

    During his playing days he won more matches against Spurs than he did against any other opponent and lost just eight of 41 encounters. As a manager, he has been beaten in only two of six meetings with the Lilywhites.

  17. A night to remember at the CBS Arenapublished at 10:59 BST 13 October
    10:59 BST 13 October

    Katie Stafford
    BBC Sport journalist

    The first instalment of Monday night football did not disappoint at the Coventry Building Society Arena as the Sky Blues put Newcastle to the sword in their first Premier League home win in over 25 years.

    From kick-off, the Sky Blues showed intensity and desire to get forward and take the game to the Magpies.

    That confidence was rewarded with an early penalty which Jack Rudoni perfectly slotted into the corner to give Coventry the lead after six minutes.

    An early goal for any team is a boost, but for a side that had failed to score until their fifth game of the campaign, it felt like the shackles were off.

    Make no mistake, it was end-to-end, with Newcastle equally as dangerous on the break. The difference being a lack of quality in their final third and Sky Blues centre-back Bobby Thomas.

    Fresh from his heroics at Nottingham Forest, the no-nonsense defender was at it again, making two crucial blocks before the break to deny Matias Fernandez-Pardo, much to the delight of the home supporters.

    Sidiki Cherif doubled the Sky Blues lead, but a rocket from Sven Botman gave Newcastle a lifeline with 20 minutes to go.

    Tired legs invited pressure, but Coventry didn't crumble and the introduction of Frank Onyeka and Victor Torp re-strengthened the midfield after Newcastle had made four changes which included Joelinton.

    Squad depth is so important in the Premier League and it showed with all of head coach Frank Lampard's changes playing a vital role in the closing stages.

    Striker Ellis Simms was called into action and scored with his first touch of the game to restore a two-goal lead, before 18-year-old Stephan Mfuni made it four after just three minutes on the pitch.

    It was an all-round performance that epitomised Lampard's philosophy of team effort.

    But more than that, back-to-back wins propel Coventry to 14th where they sit level on points with Manchester United.

    There's growing confidence that they have what it takes to compete in the Premier League.

  22. Analysis: Floodgates open for Coventrypublished at 22:31 BST 12 October
    22:31 BST 12 October

    Emily Salley
    BBC Sport journalist

    The international break did not fall kindly for Coventry and they were unable to gather momentum after defeating Nottingham Forest for their first Premier League win since the 2000-01 campaign.

    But the hosts could not have asked for a better start on Monday night as Lukas Hornicek, under pressure from Ephron Mason-Clark, recklessly parried the ball away before throwing himself at the legs of Sidiki Cherif.

    Newcastle piled on the pressure after Jack Rudoni's opener and Coventry had defender Bobby Thomas to thank for entering the break in front as he made two excellent blocks to deny Matias Fernandez-Pardo.

    After scoring just one goal in their opening five games, the floodgates finally opened for Frank Lampard's side and Cherif's piercing finish in the 48th minute ensured Coventry raced out of the blocks again in the second half.

    Simms made the most of the advantage given by referee John Brooks, who allowed play to go on after Joelinton clattered into Brandon Thomas-Asante on the edge of the box.

    But it was 18-year-old Stephen Mfuni's goal that was the pick of the bunch for the hosts as he drove into the penalty area before bending a lovely curling finish past Hornicek.

    After difficult tests against Arsenal and Manchester City to start the season, this victory will be all the evidence Lampard and Coventry fans need to prove they have what it takes not only to survive this season, but compete.

  25. Coventry v Newcastle: Key stats and talking pointspublished at 09:32 BST 12 October
    09:32 BST 12 October

    Noel Sliney
    BBC Sport senior journalist

    Coventry celebrated their first Premier League goal and points for more than 25 years by beating Nottingham Forest before the international break.

    Monday night's visitors Newcastle have won all seven league and cup meetings this century between the two, albeit the clubs haven't faced each other since February 2010.

    A pivotal October for Coventry?

    The caveat to Coventry's run of four straight league defeats from the start of the season was that it included away games against last season's top two sides. In a more favourable-looking run this month, they have three home fixtures out of four and will face the two teams who sat below them going into the latest round of matches.

    Having felled Forest, Coventry are attempting to become the first English side in 127 years to earn consecutive top-flight wins immediately after losing their opening four games without scoring. Derby County are the only club to do so, in 1899, and they rallied to finish sixth.

    A far more modern precedent to give Coventry some belief is that three promoted sides have started as badly as them in the Premier League and still avoided relegation.

    Head coach Frank Lampard hopes his team can take inspiration from the memory of their most recent Monday night home game, an outstanding 3-1 win over Middlesbrough in February. Coventry were second to Boro going into that fixture after losing a 10-point lead, but victory put them back on top and they pulled away to clinch the Championship title.

    "I watch parts of that game back regularly because it was our outstanding game last year considering what was on it," said Lampard. "And now we have to do 38 (sic) versions of that kind of feeling to get things nailed on."

    Positive start for Jaissle

    Newcastle are playing promoted opponents for the second match in a row, having held off a late fightback to end Hull City's unbeaten start to the season. That result, coupled with a high-tempo first-half display, made partial amends for the dismal 4-1 defeat at Leeds United five days earlier.

    Newcastle's defensive vulnerability is clear; they faced a league-high 97 shots across their opening five games.

    Nonetheless, a tally of eight points from five games is a solid start under Matthias Jaissle â especially in the wake of a tumultuous summer during which they sold their three highest-profile players.

    Despite injuries hindering Newcastle's youthful rebuild, a win on Monday would give Jaissle the joint-second best start by a Magpies manager after six Premier League games.
