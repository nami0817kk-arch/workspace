'''Diego Pablo Simeone González''' ({{IPA|es|ˈdjeɣo ˈpaβlo simeˈone}}; {{IPA|it|ˈdjɛːɡo simeˈoːne, ˈdjeː-|lang}};<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.dipionline.it/dizionario/ricerca?lemma=Diego|title=Diego|work=DiPI Online|author=[[Luciano Canepari]]|access-date=28 October 2018|language=it}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.dipionline.it/dizionario/ricerca?lemma=Simeone|title=Simeone|work=DiPI Online|last=Canepari|first=Luciano|access-date=28 October 2018|language=it}}</ref> born 28 April 1970), nicknamed "'''Cholo'''" ({{IPA|es|ˈtʃolo|}}), is an Argentine professional [[association football|football]] [[Manager (association football)|manager]] and former player who is the head coach of {{Spanish football updater|Atlético Madrid}} club [[Atlético Madrid]] since 2011. This makes him the second longest serving professional manager in Europe behind [[Frank Schmidt (footballer)|Frank Schmidt]], but the longest serving professional manager in Europe in [[List of top-division football clubs in UEFA countries|the top-division of their country]].

In his club career as a [[Midfielder (association football)|midfielder]] that started in 1987, Simeone played in Argentina, Italy, and Spain for [[Club Atlético Vélez Sarsfield|Vélez Sarsfield]], [[Pisa SC|Pisa]], [[Sevilla FC|Sevilla]], [[Atlético Madrid]], [[Inter Milan]], [[SS Lazio|Lazio]] and [[Racing Club de Avellaneda|Racing Club]]. He won a [[Double (association football)#Spain|domestic double]] with Atlético Madrid in 1996, and the [[UEFA Europa League|UEFA Cup]] with Inter in [[1998 UEFA Cup Final|1998]], also winning another [[Double (association football)#Italy|domestic double]] with Lazio in 2000, as well as the [[1999 UEFA Super Cup]] and the [[2000 Supercoppa Italiana]]. Simeone was [[Cap (sports)|capped]] over [[List of men's footballers with 100 or more international caps|100]] times for the [[Argentina national football team|Argentina national team]] and represented the country at the [[1994 FIFA World Cup|1994]], [[1998 FIFA World Cup|1998]], and [[2002 FIFA World Cup|2002]] [[FIFA World Cup]]s, and in four editions of the [[Copa América]], winning the tournament in [[1991 Copa América|1991]] and [[1993 Copa América|1993]]. He also won the [[FIFA Confederations Cup]] in [[1992 King Fahd Cup|1992]], the [[1993 Artemio Franchi Cup]], and a silver medal at the [[Football at the 1996 Summer Olympics – Men's tournament|1996 Summer Olympic games]].

As a manager, Simeone has coached Argentine sides Racing Club, [[Estudiantes de La Plata|Estudiantes]], [[Club Atlético River Plate|River Plate]], [[San Lorenzo de Almagro|San Lorenzo]] and Italian club [[Calcio Catania|Catania]], before joining Spanish club Atlético Madrid in 2011. He won the [[Argentine Primera División]] both with Estudiantes and River Plate. Simeone has had his biggest managerial success with Atlético Madrid, turning the team into a competitor for the La Liga and breaking the [[Real Madrid C.F|Real Madrid]]–[[FC Barcelona|Barcelona]] duopoly. He has won La Liga twice, the [[Copa del Rey]], two [[UEFA Europa League]]s, two [[UEFA Super Cup]]s, as well as being runner-up of the [[UEFA Champions League]] twice. Simeone is the longest-serving manager in La Liga, having stayed for well over a decade at Atlético. He won Atléti their first [[Madrid derby|derby]] against arch-rivals Real Madrid in fourteen years in the [[2013 Copa del Rey final]] at the [[Bernabéu (stadium)|Bernabeu]].

==Club career==
[[File:Simeone 1987.jpg|thumb|upright|left|Simeone with [[Club Atlético Vélez Sarsfield|Vélez Sarsfield]] in 1987]]
When Simeone was 14, his youth coach [[Victorio Spinetto]] nicknamed him "''[[Cholo]]''", as his energetic play reminded him of former [[Boca Juniors]] player and Argentine international [[Carmelo Simeone]] (no relation) who possessed the nickname.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.ole.com.ar/diario/2002/06/05/m-397561.htm|title=Jens Fjellström ny tränare i Malmö FF (Jens Fjellström new coach in Malmö FF)|publisher=aftonbladet.se|date=26 July 2016|access-date=6 December 2016|archive-date=16 August 2003|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20030816041947/https://www.ole.com.ar/diario/2002/06/05/m-397561.htm|url-status=dead}}</ref>

After starting his career with [[Club Atlético Vélez Sarsfield|Vélez Sarsfield]], Simeone moved to [[Pisa SC|Pisa]] in 1990. The club was relegated from [[Serie A]] in his [[1990–91 Serie A|first season]] and, after it failed to gain promotion the [[1991–92 Serie B|following year]], Simeone was sold to [[La Liga]] club [[Sevilla FC|Sevilla]]. Simeone played two seasons in Seville, after which he was signed by [[Atlético Madrid]]. At Atlético, he was part of the team which won the [[Double (association football)|double]] of the [[List of Spanish football champions|Liga title]] and [[1995–96 Copa del Rey|Copa del Rey]] during the [[1995–96 La Liga|1995–96 season]], serving as the club's [[Captain (association football)|captain]].<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.theguardian.com/football/blog/2014/mar/01/diego-simeone-atletico-madrid-real-madrid-derby-la-liga|title=Diego Simeone leads Atlético Madrid with passion into derby with Real|work=[[The Guardian]]|last=Lowe|first=Sid|date=1 March 2014|access-date=19 June 2024}}</ref>

In 1997, Simeone returned to Serie A with [[Inter Milan]],<ref>{{cite news|last=Miguélez|first=Jose|url=https://elpais.com/diario/1997/06/28/deportes/867448808_850215.html|title=Simeone ficha por el Inter|trans-title=Simeone signs for Inter|work=[[El País]]|date=27 June 1997|access-date=7 October 2021|language=es}}</ref> and played two full seasons, winning the [[1997–98 UEFA Cup]] in a side spearheaded by [[Ronaldo (Brazilian footballer)|Ronaldo]] up front.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.inter.it/en/news/2020-05-03-documentary-road-to-paris-inter-uefa-cup-1998|title=Road to Paris, the exclusive documentary on our 1998 UEFA Cup triumph|publisher=www.inter.it|date=3 May 2020|access-date=19 June 2024}}</ref> In 1999, Simeone joined fellow Argentines [[Roberto Sensini|Néstor Sensini]], [[Matías Almeyda]] and [[Juan Sebastián Verón]] at [[Sven-Göran Eriksson]]'s [[SS Lazio|Lazio]].<ref>{{cite news|work=The Guardian|last=Brodkin|first=Jon|date=9 June 1999|access-date=19 June 2024|url=https://www.theguardian.com/football/1999/jun/09/newsstory.sport1|title=Record £28m takes Vieri to Inter}}</ref> The side had gone close to the ''[[List of Italian football champions|Scudetto]]'' in the season before Simeone's arrival and he helped deliver the championship after [[1999–2000 Serie A|a season]], where [[Juventus FC|Juventus]] led the standings by two points going into the last day. A Juve loss at rainy [[AC Perugia Calcio|Perugia]] coupled with Lazio's comfortable 3–0 home win over [[Reggina 1914|Reggina]] at the [[Stadio Olimpico]] ensured Simeone's first Serie A title. After winning the double in Spain, he would then add the Italian double as Lazio edged out Inter to claim the [[1999–2000 Coppa Italia]].<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.theguardian.com/football/these-football-times/2015/mar/30/sven-goran-eriksson-lazio-serie-a-1999-2000-mancini-milan|title=How Sven-Goran Eriksson's Lazio won the Serie A title race of 1999-2000|work=The Guardian|access-date=19 June 2024|last=Newman|first=Blair|date=30 March 2015}}</ref>

He went on to play three more seasons in Rome, which included more last day drama as Simeone's goal against former club Inter on the last day of the [[2001–02 Serie A|2001–02 campaign]] effectively ruined his old employers' title dream.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.theguardian.com/football/the-gentleman-ultra/2022/may/05/juventus-inter-roma-title-serie-a-finale-history|title=When Juve pipped Inter to the title in the greatest Serie A finale in history|last=Bell|first=Henry|work=The Guardian|date=5 May 2022|access-date=19 June 2024}}</ref>

After cancelling his contract with Lazio,<ref>{{cite web|last=Diaz|first=Francisco Javier|url=https://as.com/masdeporte/2003/07/16/polideportivo/1058328886_850215.html|title=El viernes presenta al 'Cholo' Simeone|trans-title=On Friday presents the 'Cholo' Simeone|work=[[Diario AS]]|date=16 July 2003|access-date=7 October 2021|language=es}}</ref> Simeone returned to Atlético Madrid in 2003,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://as.com/futbol/2003/07/16/mas_futbol/1058306424_850215.html|title=Simeone: "Sabía que volvería"|trans-title=Simeone: "I knew I would return"|work=Diario AS|date=16 July 2003|access-date=7 October 2021|language=es}}</ref> spending his next two seasons there. In total, he played in 165 matches for Atlético, scoring 31 goals. In December 2004, it was confirmed that he would leave Europe and return to Argentina to finish his playing career with [[Racing Club de Avellaneda|Racing Club]].<ref>{{cite news|last=Torres|first=Diego|url=https://elpais.com/diario/2004/12/11/deportes/1102719605_850215.html|title=Simeone vuelve a dejar el Atlético|trans-title=Simeone leaves Atlético again|work=[[El País]]|date=10 December 2004|access-date=7 October 2021|language=es}}</ref>

==Style of play==
Simeone was regarded as a tenacious, versatile, hard-working and complete two-way [[midfielder]] who was mobile, good in the air, and capable both of winning balls and starting attacking plays, while also having a penchant for scoring several goals himself. ==Managerial career==
===Early years===
Simeone ended his playing career for Racing Club, playing his last match on 17 February 2006, and then became manager for the same team. After a rough start, the team made an impressive finish in the 2006 Clausura. When a new club president was elected, Simeone left Racing in May 2006 and was replaced by [[Reinaldo Merlo]].

On 18 May, Simeone became head coach of [[Estudiantes de La Plata|Estudiantes]] and soon led them to their first [[List of Argentine football champions|league title]] in 23 years after defeating Boca Juniors 2–1 in a final match played on 13 December 2006. In an October 2006 poll in the sports daily ''[[Olé (sports newspaper)|Olé]]'', Simeone was voted as the best manager in the Argentine league.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.clarin.com/diario/2006/10/31/um/m-01300708.htm|title=Simeone, el gran estratega del fútbol argentino|last=Lagares|first=Juan|publisher=Clarin|date=31 October 2006|access-date=10 November 2006|archive-date=17 June 2009|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090617171043/https://www.clarin.com/diario/2006/10/31/um/m-01300708.htm|url-status=dead}}</ref> He was also praised as a "born manager" by former Argentine international [[Roberto Perfumo]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://old.ole.clarin.com/jsp/v4/pagina.jsp?pagId=01309221|archive-url=https://archive.today/20080108095141/https://old.ole.clarin.com/jsp/v4/pagina.jsp?pagId=01309221|url-status=dead|archive-date=8 January 2008|title=El técnico se hace, sí, pero sobre todo nace|publisher=Clarin|last=Perfumo|first=Roberto}}</ref> Simeone left Estudiantes after the end of the 2007 Apertura, where Estudiantes was not a contender after a poor start, but had a strong finish of nine games without defeat. On 15 December 2007, Simeone was unveiled as the new [[Club Atlético River Plate|River Plate]] coach, succeeding [[Daniel Passarella]]. The contract was reported to be for one year, starting on 3 January 2008.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.reuters.com/article/sports/soccer/simeone-to-coach-argentinas-river-plate-idUSN15329184/|title=Simeone to coach Argentina's River Plate|agency=[[Reuters]]|date=15 December 2007|access-date=24 February 2026}}</ref> After an early elimination in the [[Copa Libertadores]], losing to [[Club Atlético San Lorenzo de Almagro|San Lorenzo]] in the second round, Simeone and River Plate went on to win the [[2007–08 Argentine Primera División season#Torneo Clausura|2008 Clausura]] championship after beating [[Club Olimpo|Olimpo]] 2–1 in the [[Estadio Monumental Antonio Vespucio Liberti|Monumental]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.iht.com/articles/ap/2008/06/09/sports/LA-SPT-SOC-Argentine-Roundup.php|title=River Plate crowned champion of Argentine Clausura|work=International Herald Tribune|date=29 March 2009}}</ref> On 7 November 2008, Simeone announced his resignation as coach of River Plate after their elimination in the quarter-finals of the [[2008 Copa Sudamericana]] by Mexican side [[C.D. Guadalajara|Chivas]] and a poor run of form of 11 domestic matches without a win, which left them bottom of the [[Primera División Argentina]] with only six matches remaining.<ref>[https://espndeportes.espn.com/news/story?id=744488&s=arg&type=story Diego Simeone renunció a la dirección técnica de River] at [[ESPN Deportes]] {{in lang|es}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://msn.foxsports.com/fsi/fsla/story/revista?contentId=8792932|title=Las causas de una salida inevitable |publisher=Msn.foxsports.com}}{{dead link|date=November 2016|bot=InternetArchiveBot|fix-attempted=yes}}</ref> On 15 April 2009, Simeone joined San Lorenzo to replace [[Miguel Ángel Russo]] following the club's exit in the first round of the [[2009 Copa Libertadores]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://soccernet.espn.go.com/news/story?id=637488&sec=global&cc=5739|title=Ex-River boss Simeone to manage San Lorenzo|publisher=[[ESPN]]|date=16 April 2009|access-date=16 April 2009|archive-date=25 May 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110525215142/https://soccernet.espn.go.com/news/story?id=637488&sec=global&cc=5739|url-status=dead }}</ref> On 3 April 2010, Simeone resigned from San Lorenzo due to poor results and mounting criticism.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://soccernet.espn.go.com/news/story?id=765971&sec=global&cc=5739|title=Simeone quits San Lorenzo post after dismal run|publisher=[[ESPN]]|date=4 April 2010|access-date=4 April 2010|archive-date=26 December 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111226030623/http://soccernet.espn.go.com/news/story?id=765971&sec=global&cc=5739|url-status=dead}}</ref>

===Catania and Racing Club===
On 19 January 2011, Simeone flew to [[Sicily]] to join Serie A side [[Calcio Catania|Catania]], replacing [[Marco Giampaolo]], who left the club just hours earlier.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.tuttomercatoweb.com/?action=read&id=248797|title=ESCLUSIVA TMW – Criscitiello: "Colpo Lo Monaco: Simeone a Catania"|trans-title=TMW EXCLUSIVE – Criscitiello: "Lo Monaco strikes: Simeone to Catania"|work=Tutto Mercato Web|date=19 January 2011|access-date=19 January 2011|language=it}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.calciocatania.it/comunicati/comunicati.php?id=21846|publisher=Calcio Catania|language=it|access-date=19 January 2011|date=19 January 2011|title=Diego Pablo Simeone è il nuovo allenatore del Catania|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110727105119/https://www.calciocatania.it/comunicati/comunicati.php?id=21846|archive-date=27 July 2011}}</ref> On 1 June 2011, Simeone left his post after helping Catania stave off relegation.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.tuttomercatoweb.com/?action=read&id=271540|title=Catania, rescinde Simeone|trans-title=Simeone quits Catania|work=Tutto Mercato Web|date=1 June 2011|access-date=1 June 2011|language=it}}</ref> On 21 June 2011, Simeone was named as the new coach of Racing Club for a second spell in charge, replacing Miguel Ángel Russo, who had resigned the week prior.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.racingclub.com.ar/detalle/noticias/2789/|title=Diego Simeone fue presentado como nuevo técnico de Racing Club|trans-title=Diego Simeone was introduced as new coach of Racing Club|work=[[Racing Club de Avellaneda|Racing]]|date=21 June 2011|access-date=21 June 2011|language=es|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110625133750/https://www.racingclub.com.ar/detalle/noticias/2789|archive-date=25 June 2011}}</ref>

===Atlético Madrid===
====2011–13: Europa League and Copa del Rey triumphs====
On 23 December 2011, Simeone was unveiled as the new [[Atlético Madrid]] coach, succeeding [[Gregorio Manzano]], who had been dismissed the day before following defeat to third-tier [[Albacete Balompié|Albacete]] in the [[Copa del Rey]]. His first game as Atlético manager was a 0–0 away draw against [[Manuel Pellegrini]]'s [[Malaga CF|Malaga]]. His first season ended with the team winning the [[2011–12 UEFA Europa League|UEFA Europa League]] by beating [[Athletic Bilbao]] 3–0 in the [[2012 UEFA Europa League Final|final]] in [[Bucharest]].<ref>{{cite news|url=https://eurosport.yahoo.com/09052012/58/europa-league-falcao-inspires-atletico-europa-crown.html|title=Europa League – Falcao inspires Atletico to Europa crown|work=[[EuroSport]]|date=9 May 2012|access-date=9 May 2012|publisher=[[Yahoo!]]|last=Quarrell|first=Dan|archive-url=https://archive.today/20120716185450/http://eurosport.yahoo.com/news/matchpack-atletico-madrid-v-athletic-174907897.html|archive-date=16 July 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref>

On 31 August 2012, his Atlético side won the [[2012 UEFA Super Cup|UEFA Super Cup]] after defeating [[Chelsea F.C.|Chelsea]] 4–1 at the [[Stade Louis II]] in Monaco. In the [[2012–13 La Liga|domestic league]], the team made an impressive start to the season, finishing the first half in second place, behind only [[FC Barcelona|Barcelona]] and above city rivals [[Real Madrid C.F|Real Madrid]]. Eventually, Simeone led the team to a third-place finish in the league, which at the time was the best finish in 17 years for Atlético. On 17 May 2013, he won the [[2012–13 Copa del Rey|Copa del Rey]] after Atlético defeated rivals Real Madrid 2–1 at the [[Santiago Bernabéu Stadium|Santiago Bernabéu]].

====2013–17: La Liga win and Champions League finals====
[[File:Atlético de Madrid, Liga 2013-2014 (Mayo 2014),.JPG|thumb|Atlético Madrid celebrates after winning the [[2013–14 La Liga]]. Simeone can be seen in the back of the photo.]]
Atlético began the [[2013–14 Atlético Madrid season|2013–14 season]] with a similar squad to their prior season, despite selling star player [[Radamel Falcao]] to [[AS Monaco FC|Monaco]] for a reported €60&nbsp;million fee. They also acquired [[David Villa]] from Barcelona on a free transfer.<ref>{{cite news|date=25 May 2021|title=Five players who crossed a fierce divide to win a league title|url=https://www.marca.com/en/football/international-football/2021/05/26/60ad73acca4741cc1b8b45a5.html|access-date=24 February 2026|work=Marca|last=Clancy|first=Connor}}</ref> While the season began with a defeat to ''Barça'' in the [[2013 Supercopa de España]], the team recorded eight-straight victories in [[2013–14 La Liga|La Liga]], the best league start in club history. This included a 1–0 away win at the Santiago Bernabéu against Real Madrid, making Simeone the first Atlético manager since [[Claudio Ranieri]] in 1999 to record a league victory there. Atlético finished the first half of the season in first place in La Liga, level on points with Barcelona at 47. In [[Barcelona 1–1 Atlético Madrid (May 2014)|the last round of the season]], on 17 May, Atlético needed at least a draw in the [[Camp Nou]] against Barcelona to be crowned champions for the first time since 1996, while a loss would give the title to Barcelona. A [[Diego Godín]] header from a corner kick in the 48th minute gave Atlético an equaliser and the draw they needed to win their tenth league title and first since 1996, when Simeone himself was an Atlético player.

Simeone became the second Argentine manager after [[Helenio Herrera]] to hand Atlético a Spanish championship, and the second manager after [[Luis Aragonés]] to win it both as a player and as a coach of the team. Under Simeone, Atlético collected 90 points in La Liga, surpassing its 1996 record of 87, making the 2013–14 season the most successful in club history.<ref>{{cite news|last=Martin|first=Richard|title=Barcelona 1 Atlético Madrid 1, La Liga: match report|url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/sport/football/competitions/la-liga/10838338/Barcelona-v-Atletico-Madrid-La-Liga-live.html|access-date=17 May 2014|newspaper=[[The Daily Telegraph]]|date=17 May 2014}}</ref>

Also in 2013–14, Atlético finished top of their [[2013–14 UEFA Champions League|Champions League]] group and qualified for the quarter-finals with a 5–1 aggregate win over [[AC Milan|Milan]] in the round of 16. This was the first time they had reached the Champions League quarter-finals since [[1996–97 UEFA Champions League|1996–97]], when Simeone played for the team. In the quarter-finals, Atlético defeated Barcelona 2–1 on aggregate from Simeone's tactic of cutting the swift-passing midfield of Barcelona in two, thus denying them space and isolating [[Xavi (footballer, born 1980)|Xavi]] and [[Andrés Iniesta]] from forwards Lionel Messi and [[Neymar]]. In the semi-finals, Atlético defeated [[José Mourinho]]'s Chelsea 3–1 at [[Stamford Bridge (stadium)|Stamford Bridge]] following a goalless draw at home to reach the [[2014 UEFA Champions League Final|Champions League final]] for only the second time in club history, the first being in [[1974 European Cup Final|1974]].<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.theguardian.com/football/2014/apr/30/chelsea-atletico-madrid-champions-league-match-report|title=Chelsea bow out to superior firepower of Atlético Madrid|date=30 April 2014|work=The Guardian|access-date=2 May 2014}}</ref> Atlético was the only undefeated team in the Champions League prior to the final, recording nine wins and three draws, and had the best defence in the competition, conceding only 6 goals in 12 matches.

In the [[2014 UEFA Champions League Final|final]] on 24 May 2014, Atlético faced [[Madrid Derby|city rivals]] Real Madrid at the [[Estádio da Luz]] in [[Lisbon]]. Despite leading from Diego Godín's header, Atlético conceded a late equaliser in the 93rd minute of the match. The goal hampered Atlético's morale, and the team ended up losing 4–1 after extra time, with Simeone losing the opportunity to be the third Argentine coach to win the Champions League, after [[Luis Carniglia]] and Helenio Herrera. After the final goal, Real Madrid's [[Raphaël Varane]] kicked the ball towards Simeone, causing Simeone to run onto the pitch in anger. He was sent to the stands, and Varane was booked for the incident. Reflecting, Simeone said, "I also made a mistake with my reaction. He's a young guy with a bright future."<ref>{{cite news|title=Simeone squares up to Varane|url=https://www.marca.com/2014/05/25/en/football/spanish_football/1400974294.html|access-date=25 May 2014|newspaper=[[Marca (newspaper)|Marca]]|date=25 May 2014}}</ref> Simeone also admitted a mistake in selecting striker [[Diego Costa]] to start the match, as he had been recently injured and was forced off after eight minutes.<ref>{{cite news|title=Atletico Madrid: Simeone admits to mistake over injured Costa|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/27562716|access-date=3 June 2014|work=[[BBC Sport]]|date=25 May 2014}}</ref>

In the summer of 2014, Chelsea acquired [[Diego Costa]], [[Filipe Luís]] and [[Thibaut Courtois]] from Atlético, and also David Villa moved to the [[Major League Soccer|MLS]]. In response, Atlético acquired striker [[Mario Mandžukić]] from [[FC Bayern Munich|Bayern Munich]], goalkeeper [[Jan Oblak]] from [[S.L. Benfica|Benfica]] and forwards [[Antoine Griezmann]] from [[Real Sociedad]] and [[Ángel Correa]] from [[San Lorenzo de Almagro|San Lorenzo]], and recovered young midfielder [[Saúl (footballer, born 1994)|Saúl]] who was on loan at [[Rayo Vallecano]]. The season started with Atlético defeating city rivals Real Madrid for the [[2014 Supercopa de España|Spanish Super Cup]] in August and repeating the previous year's victory in the Bernabéu for the league in September. In January, the team acquired [[Fernando Torres]] on loan from Milan. However, Atlético finished the league in third place and was eliminated in the [[2014–15 UEFA Champions League|UEFA Champions League]] quarter-finals by Real Madrid 1–0, on aggregate.

Before the start of the 2015–16 season, Filipe Luís returned from Chelsea with the team also acquiring Montenegrin defender [[Stefan Savić]] and Belgian midfielder [[Yannick Carrasco]]. Mario Mandžukić left for [[Juventus FC|Juventus]]. In 2015–16, Atlético was the team with the best defensive record in Europe's "big five" leagues, only allowing 18 goals scored against them in 38 La Liga games. Atlético was also the team with the most clean sheets in their games. The team was contending the [[2015–16 La Liga|league title]] until the last fixture against Barcelona and Real Madrid, settling for third with 88 points.

In the [[2015–16 UEFA Champions League|UEFA Champions League]], Simeone led Atlético to their second Champions League final in three seasons, becoming the first Argentine manager since [[Héctor Cúper]] to reach two Champions League finals. Atlético had defeated [[PSV Eindhoven|PSV]] on penalties during the round of 16, beat defending champions Barcelona 3–2 on aggregate and favourites Bayern Munich 2–2, going through on away goals, setting up a repeat of the 2014 final again facing Real Madrid. The match ended 1–1 after extra time leading to a penalty shootout. [[Juanfran (footballer, born 1985)|Juanfran]] was the only player to miss his kick, enabling [[Cristiano Ronaldo]] to score Real's last spot kick, condemning Atlético to a second Champions League final defeat in three seasons.

[[File:Diego Pablo Simeone en la presentación de la promoción de la estación Estadio Metropolitano.jpg|thumb|upright|Simeone in 2017]]
In the summer of 2016, Atlético purchased forward [[Kevin Gameiro]] from [[Sevilla FC|Sevilla]], midfielder [[Nicolás Gaitán]] from Benfica, and defender [[Šime Vrsaljko]] from [[US Sassuolo|Sassuolo]]. They also acquired the rights to Fernando Torres (previously on loan from Milan). The team again finished third in the [[2016–17 La Liga|league]] and lost in the [[2016–17 UEFA Champions League|UEFA Champions League]] semi-finals, again to Real Madrid.

====2017–21: Second Europa League and La Liga victories====
On 5 September 2017, Simeone extended his contract with Atlético for two more years, until June 2020.<ref>{{cite news|title=Diego Simeone: Atletico Madrid manager signs two-year contract extension|url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/football/41161373|work=BBC Sport|date=5 September 2017|access-date=24 February 2026}}</ref> In the [[2017–18 Atlético Madrid season|2017–18 season]], Atlético would finish in second place in La Liga, behind Barcelona but ahead of city rivals Real Madrid.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.reuters.com/article/uk-soccer-spain-idUKKCN1ID0SS|title=Atletico clinch second spot, Bale leads Real rout|work=Reuters|first1=Richard|last1=Martin|first2=Ed|last2=Osmond|access-date=18 May 2018|language=en-GB}}</ref>

In the [[2017–18 UEFA Champions League group stage]], Atlético did not advance and dropped to the [[2017–18 UEFA Europa League]]. In January 2018, Diego Costa returned to Atlético from Chelsea. On 16 May 2018, the team won the [[2018 UEFA Europa League Final]] against [[Olympique de Marseille|Marseille]].<ref>{{cite news|last=Malagón|first=Manuel|title=Simeone gana su sexto título como técnico del Atlético|url=https://www.marca.com/futbol/atletico/2018/05/16/5afc6a92e5fdeab9488b45f1.html|date=16 May 2018|access-date=16 May 2018|newspaper=Marca|publisher=[[Unidad Editorial]] Información Deportiva, S.L.U.|language=es}}</ref> Simeone was sent to the stands in the first leg of the semi-finals against [[Arsenal F.C.|Arsenal]] and was suspended. He was forced to watch the second leg and final from the stands.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.espn.com/soccer/story/_/id/37553282/diego-simeone-handed-europa-league-final-touchline-ban|title=Simeone banned from Europa final touchline|publisher=ESPN|access-date=18 May 2018}}</ref>

In the 2018–19 season, Atlético bought [[Thomas Lemar]] from [[AS Monaco|Monaco]]<ref>{{cite web|url=https://bleacherreport.com/articles/2708864-thomas-lemar-transfer-agreement-with-atletico-madrid-announced-by-monaco|title=Thomas Lemar Transfer Agreement with Atletico Madrid Announced by Monaco|last=Marsden|first=Rory|website=Bleacher Report}}</ref> and [[Rodri (footballer, born 1996)|Rodri]] from [[Villarreal CF|Villarreal]],<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.mirror.co.uk/sport/football/transfer-news/breaking-rodri-completes-628million-transfer-17389172|title=Spain midfielder Rodri completes £62.8million transfer to Manchester City|last=Richards|first=Alex|date=4 July 2019|work=The Mirror}}</ref> and in January 2019 acquired [[Álvaro Morata]] on loan from Chelsea,<ref>{{cite web|date=28 January 2019|access-date=24 February 2026|url=https://www.skysports.com/football/news/11668/11620554/alvaro-morata-joins-atletico-madrid-from-chelsea-on-loan|title=Alvaro Morata joins Atletico Madrid from Chelsea on loan|website=Sky Sports}}</ref> while longtime team captain Gabi signed for Qatari club [[Al-Sadd SC|Al-Sadd]] before the start of the season.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.sbs.com.au/sport/article/atletico-captain-gabi-agrees-to-join-qatars-al-sadd/ogneei8cz|title=Atletico captain Gabi agrees to join Qatar's Al Sadd|agency=[[SBS (Australian TV channel)|SBS]]|date=4 July 2018|access-date=24 February 2026}}</ref> They started the season by winning the [[2018 UEFA Super Cup]] against arch-rivals, [[Real Madrid CF|Real Madrid]], winning 4-2 after extra time.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Murray |first=Scott |date=2018-08-15 |title=Real Madrid 2-4 Atlético Madrid (aet; 2-2 after 90 mins): Uefa Super Cup – as it happened |url=http://www.theguardian.com/football/live/2018/aug/15/real-madrid-v-atletico-madrid-uefa-super-cup-live |access-date=2026-10-05 |work=the Guardian |language=en-GB |issn=0261-3077}}</ref> The team again finished second in the [[2018–19 La Liga|league]] behind Barcelona and ahead of city rivals Real Madrid. In the [[2018–19 UEFA Champions League|Champions League]], they advanced to the first knockout round, where they were eliminated by [[Juventus]], winning the first leg 2–0 at the [[Metropolitano Stadium|Wanda Metropolitano]] but losing the return leg 3–0 in Turin.

Simeone guided his side into the last 16 of [[UEFA Champions League]] for the sixth time since he came to reign in 2011, while before his arrival [[Atlético Madrid|Atlético]] had only done so seven times in 60 years.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.marca.com/en/football/spanish-football/2019/12/12/5df2969422601d85748b4649.html|title=Simeone's Long Lasting Atlético Madrid|last=Suárez|first=Isaac|work=Marca|date=12 December 2019|access-date=24 February 2026}}</ref> On 11 March 2020, Atlético Madrid eliminated the reigning European champions Liverpool with a 3–2 triumph after extra-time at [[Anfield]], winning 4–2 on aggregate, after [[Saúl (footballer, born 1994)|Saúl]] secured a 1–0 first-leg win in the reverse via a 5th-minute strike.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/football/51800660|title=Liverpool 2–3 Atletico Madrid (2–4 agg): Holders out of Champions League|date=11 March 2020|work=BBC Sport|last=McNulty|first=Phil|access-date=24 February 2026|language=en-GB}}</ref>
On 27 June 2020, Simeone won his 195th game in the top-flight of Spanish football as Atleti beat [[Deportivo Alavés|Alavés]] 2–1 on Saturday. With the triumph, the Argentine has overtaken the club legend, [[Luis Aragonés]] (194), as the manager with the most [[La Liga]] wins in the club's history.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://en.atleticodemadrid.com/noticias/simeone-becomes-atleti-coach-with-most-top-flight-wins|title=Simeone becomes Atleti coach with most top-flight wins|website=Atlético de Madrid|date=27 June 2020|access-date=24 February 2026}}</ref>

On 7 July 2020, Simeone overtook [[John Toshack]] as the third coach with the most games managed at a single club in the history of the top-flight of Spanish football during Atleti's match against [[RC Celta de Vigo|Celta Vigo]], his 323rd game in La Liga. Only [[Miguel Muñoz]] ([[Real Madrid CF|Real Madrid]], 424) and, the club legend, [[Luis Aragonés]] (407) remain ahead of him.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://en.atleticodemadrid.com/noticias/simeone-becomes-third-coach-with-most-games-at-single-club-in-spain-top-flight|website=Atlético de Madrid|title=Simeone becomes third coach with most games at single club in Spain top-flight|date=7 July 2020|access-date=24 February 2026}}</ref>

[[File:Diego Simeone.jpg|thumb|upright|Simeone in 2019]]

For the [[2020–21 Atlético Madrid season|2020–21 season]], Atlético parted ways with longtime-serving forward [[Diego Costa]] and acquired [[Luis Suárez (Uruguayan footballer)|Luis Suárez]] after his departure from Barcelona. The team brought back [[Yannick Carrasco]] from [[Dalian Professional F.C.|Dalian Professional]] and also acquired [[Geoffrey Kondogbia]] from [[Valencia CF|Valencia]], [[Lucas Torreira]] on loan from [[Arsenal F.C.|Arsenal]] and [[Moussa Dembélé (French footballer)|Moussa Dembelé]] on loan from [[Olympique Lyonnais|Lyon]], while [[Thomas Partey]] left for Arsenal and [[Álvaro Morata]] was loaned to [[Juventus FC|Juventus]]. On 17 October 2020, Simeone reached his 200th win in the league in a 2–0 away win against [[RC Celta de Vigo|Celta Vigo]].<ref>{{cite news|url=https://en.atleticodemadrid.com/noticias/simeone-reaches-200-laliga-wins-2|title=Simeone reaches 200 LaLiga wins|website=Atlético de Madrid|date=17 October 2020|access-date=24 February 2026}}</ref> On 30 December 2020, he reached his 500th match in charge of Atlético Madrid in a 1–0 win over [[Getafe CF|Getafe]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.goal.com/en/news/simeone-500-game-milestone-in-charge-atletico-madrid/1wuqpniap60xt1ugsc12bfjbvz|title=Simeone reaches milestone with 500th game in charge of Atletico Madrid|website=Goal.com|date=30 December 2020|access-date=31 December 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201230174033/https://www.goal.com/en/news/simeone-500-game-milestone-in-charge-atletico-madrid/1wuqpniap60xt1ugsc12bfjbvz|archive-date=30 December 2020|url-status=dead}}</ref>
On 10 March 2021, Simeone overtook [[Luis Aragonés]] for the most wins ever as manager of the club in a 2–1 home win against [[Athletic Bilbao]], his 309th win overall.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.beinsports.com/us/laliga/video/simeone-reaches-record-breaking-win-at-atleti/1651002|title=Simeone Surpasses Aragones' Win Record as Atletico Madrid Boss|website=beIN Sports|date=3 October 2021|access-date=24 February 2026}}</ref>

After a 4–0 away defeat to [[FC Bayern Munich|Bayern Munich]] and many injuries to key players in November 2020, Simeone modified the formation of his squad by introducing a three-man defence for the first time in his tenure coaching Atlético. In this formation, Carrasco played as a wing-back, with left centre-back [[Mario Hermoso]] being instructed to also cover the left-back position when Carrasco moved forward, which made the formation tactically flexible between a three-man and four-man defence.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://totalfootballanalysis.com/match-analysis/la-liga-202021-atletico-madrid-vs-barcelona-tactical-analysis-tactics|last=Leveridge|first=Sam|title=La Liga 2020/21: Atletico Madrid vs Barcelona - tactical analysis|date=24 November 2020|access-date=24 February 2026}}</ref> After the formational change, Atlético were solidified defensively and began a streak of victories, including a 1–0 home victory against Barcelona. This streak propelled the team to a ten-point lead over Real Madrid and Barcelona in the [[2020–21 La Liga]] title race by February. However, a series of bad results from February to April meant that Atlético were eliminated from the [[2020–21 UEFA Champions League|Champions League]] by [[Chelsea F.C.|Chelsea]] and Real Madrid closed the league gap to two points before the final matchday, meaning Atlético needed a victory in the last La Liga round to win the championship. Thanks to a Suárez goal in the 67th minute, Atlético Madrid managed eventually to win their second La Liga title under Simeone, after a 2–1 away win over [[Real Valladolid]] on the final match day.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.theguardian.com/football/2021/may/22/real-valladolid-atletico-madrid-la-liga-match-report|title=Atlético Madrid clinch La Liga title after Luis Suárez seals win at Real Valladolid|work=The Guardian|date=22 May 2021|access-date=24 February 2026|last=Lowe|first=Sid}}</ref>

On 8 July 2021, Atleti announced their extension to Simeone's contract until 2024, and in the same announcement it was also made known that the members of his coaching staff had also renewed their contracts.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://en.atleticodemadrid.com/noticias/simeone-signs-contract-extension-until-2024|title=Simeone signs contract extension until 2024|publisher=[[Atlético Madrid]]|date=8 July 2021|access-date=9 July 2021}}</ref>

====2022–present: Contract extension and new records====
On 9 November 2023, Simeone extended his contract with Atlético Madrid until 30 June 2027.<ref>{{cite web|date=9 November 2023|title=Club Atlético de Madrid - Simeone renews until 2027|url=https://en.atleticodemadrid.com/noticias/simeone-renews-until-2027|access-date=10 November 2023|publisher=Club Atlético de Madrid}}</ref> Later that month, on 28 November, he managed his [[European Cup and UEFA Champions League records and statistics#All-time managerial appearances|100th Champions League match]] in a 3–1 away victory over [[Feyenoord]], becoming the third manager to achieve this feat with one club following [[Alex Ferguson]] and [[Arsène Wenger]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://en.atleticodemadrid.com/noticias/diego-pablo-simeone-celebrates-100-champions-league-appearances|title=Diego Pablo Simeone celebrates 100 Champions League appearances|publisher=Atlético Madrid|date=28 November 2023|access-date=24 February 2026}}</ref> On 23 November 2024, he managed his 700th match with the club in all competitions in a 2–1 victory over [[Deportivo Alavés|Alavés]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.beinsports.com/en-us/soccer/la-liga/articles-video/simeone-celebrates-his-700th-match-with-atl%C3%A9tico-madrid-with-an-epic-comeback-2024-11-23|title=Simeone Celebrates His 700th Match with Atlético Madrid with an Epic Comeback|publisher=beIN SPORTS|date=23 November 2024|access-date=24 February 2026}}</ref> On 2 May 2026, Simeone reached his 1000th career match as a manager in a 2–0 away win over Valencia.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://en.atleticodemadrid.com/noticias/simeone-reaches-1-000-matches-as-a-manager |title=Simeone reaches 1,000 matches as a manager |publisher=Atlético Madrid |date=3 May 2026 }}</ref>

==Style of management==
[[File:Diego Simeone - 01.jpg|Simeone in 2013|thumb|upright]]
Simeone's preferred formation is the [[Formation (association football)#4–4–2|4–4–2]], which he first practised when he was manager of [[Estudiantes de la Plata|Estudiantes]] in 2006. In this formation, the two wingers move inside and act like attacking midfielders, creating space for the full backs and fluidity in the attack, effectively being a 4–2–2–2 when the team is on the offensive. The two holding midfielders provide the defensive muscle needed to win midfield battles. Utilizing this formation, Simeone's Estudiantes won the [[2006–07 Argentine Primera División|2006 Apertura]] by beating [[Boca Juniors]] 2–1 while being down to ten men from the first half. It is essentially this formation that Simeone also utilizes at Atlético Madrid. Simeone has listed his coaching influences being [[Marcelo Bielsa]], [[Sven-Göran Eriksson]], [[Alfio Basile]], [[Victorio Spinetto]], [[Luigi Simoni]], [[Carlos Bilardo]] and [[Radomir Antić]].<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://thesefootballtimes.co/2016/07/04/investigating-the-origins-of-cholismo/|title=Investigating the origins of Diego Simeone's Cholismo|access-date=28 September 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180929000510/https://thesefootballtimes.co/2016/07/04/investigating-the-origins-of-cholismo/|archive-date=29 September 2018|url-status=dead}}</ref>

When he was manager at River Plate, a team graced with the attacking talents of [[Radamel Falcao]], [[Alexis Sánchez]], [[Ariel Ortega]], [[Mauro Rosales]], [[Diego Buonanotte]], [[Augusto Fernández]] and [[Sebastián Abreu]], Simeone played an aggressive 3–3–1–3 formation reminiscent of those used by influential manager (and Simeone's mentor) [[Marcelo Bielsa]].<ref name=mirror>{{cite news|url=https://www.mirror.co.uk/sport/football/news/how-finishing-bottom-argentine-league-8062543|title=How finishing bottom of the Argentine league helped make Diego Simeone the manager he is today|last=Malyon|first=Ed|date=27 May 2016|work=The Mirror|access-date=24 February 2026}}</ref> Using this formation, River won the [[2007–08 Argentine Primera División|2008 Clausura]], but in the next season, with Sánchez having returned to [[Udinese]] and several players suffering from injuries, the 3–3–1–3 backfired and River finished bottom of the table, with Simeone being sacked in the process.<ref name=mirror/>

A common feature of Simeone's teams is defensive compactness and quick counter-attacks. Their defending consists of two stages: deep defending in their own defensive third in a narrow 4–4–2 consisting of two closely connected defensive lines of four players, and counter-pressing in their opponents' third to win the ball high up the pitch. When the ball is won, the team attack as a unit, deploying their fluid 4–2–2–2 formation, with the forwards often shifting to the flanks to create even more space.<ref>{{cite web|date=10 February 2015|title=Atletico Madrid 4–0 Real Madrid: Atletico solid in central areas when defending and fluid to cause overloads out wide when attacking|url=http://www.zonalmarking.net/2015/02/10/atletico-madrid-4-0-real-madrid-atletico-solid-in-central-areas-when-defending-and-fluid-to-cause-overloads-out-wide-when-attacking/|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150212073823/http://www.zonalmarking.net/2015/02/10/atletico-madrid-4-0-real-madrid-atletico-solid-in-central-areas-when-defending-and-fluid-to-cause-overloads-out-wide-when-attacking/|archive-date=12 February 2015|access-date=23 April 2021|website=www.zonalmarking.net}}</ref><ref name=auto>{{cite web|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5j6ZT_NFJ8U|title=Atletico Madrid Defending Analysis - YouTube|website=www.youtube.com|date=18 May 2014|access-date=24 February 2026}}{{cbignore}}</ref> In defense, the key idea is to force opponents into wide areas, gain numerical dominance in the ball's zone and steal the ball or force the opponent into a backward pass.<ref name=auto/> Counter-pressing consists of reducing an opposing team's space, disrupting their build-up and reducing the number of "safe" positions for opponents' movement on the pitch, thus eliminating goal threats.<ref name=auto/> [[Claudio Ranieri]]'s [[Premier League]]-winning [[2015–16 Leicester City F.C. season|2015–16 Leicester City]] team has been compared to Simeone's Atletico Madrid for upsetting bigger teams by also playing a narrow 4–4–2 and being compact in defense and midfield.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.goal.com/en/news/1862/premier-league/2016/04/27/22851442/filipe-luis-leicester-are-like-atletico|title=Filipe Luis: Leicester are like Atletico &#124; Goal.com|website=www.goal.com|date=27 April 2016|access-date=28 September 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180929041107/https://www.goal.com/en/news/1862/premier-league/2016/04/27/22851442/filipe-luis-leicester-are-like-atletico|archive-date=29 September 2018|url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.theguardian.com/football/2017/apr/07/atletico-madrid-title-win-leicester-president|title=Atlético Madrid's title win was just like Leicester's, says president|last=Aarons|first=Ed|date=7 April 2017|work=The Guardian|access-date=24 February 2026}}</ref>

In the short time he managed Italian side Catania in 2011, he switched between a 4–2–3–1 and a 4–3–1–2 in order to bring out the best from playmaker [[Adrián Ricchiuti]] while employing many of the counter-attacking features that would later become his trademark at Atlético. Ricchiuti was tasked with linking the midfield and attack and creating chances for whichever of [[Maxi López]], [[Gonzalo Bergessio]] and [[Francesco Lodi]] were selected up front. Simeone said he did not want to "fossilise [himself] with a rigid tactic", saying managers cannot have a favourite formation "for the simple fact that we have to adapt not to what we like but to what we have to work with".<ref name=guardian>{{cite news|url=https://www.theguardian.com/football/these-football-times/2019/feb/20/diego-simeone-italy-catania-atletico-madrid|title=The five months in Italy that shaped Diego Simeone's managerial career|last=Lea|first=Greg|date=20 February 2019|work=The Guardian|access-date=24 February 2026}}</ref> His players stayed compact and narrow when defending, with the wide men – two of [[Ezequiel Schelotto]], [[Giuseppe Mascara]], [[Raphael Martinho]] and [[Papu Gómez|Alejandro Gómez]] – expected to drop back and tuck in when out of possession; meanwhile, playmaker Ricchiuti and the central striker – more often than not López – moved back into midfield rather than pressing the centre-backs, with the aim being preventing simple passes into the middle of the pitch.<ref name=guardian/>

Simeone is also renowned for his attention to detail and quick adaptability to a game's changing situations. During his time as manager of Atlético Madrid, he has placed emphasis on creating chances from set-pieces, while not allowing the opponent to create chances from [[Set piece (football)|set-pieces]]. Several important goals, including the one that gave Atlético the 2014 La Liga title, have been scored from set-pieces.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.zonalmarking.net/2014/05/19/barcelona-1-1-atletico-simeones-side-seal-historic-title-victory-at-the-camp-nou/|title=Barcelona 1–1 Atletico: Simeone's side seal historic title victory at the Camp Nou|date=9 May 2014|access-date=28 September 2018|archive-date=29 September 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180929000326/https://www.zonalmarking.net/2014/05/19/barcelona-1-1-atletico-simeones-side-seal-historic-title-victory-at-the-camp-nou/|url-status=dead}}</ref> While his teams aren't known for controlling possession, they are lauded for controlling the pace and space in which a game is played. He is quick in making tactical changes to influence a game's outcome. In the [[2015–16 UEFA Champions League]] semi-final against Bayern in Munich, with his team down 1–0 after 45 minutes, he moved [[Saúl Ñíguez]] from the right wing to a holding midfielder position, changing the 4–4–2 to a 4–1–4–1 formation with [[Yannick Carrasco]] and [[Antoine Griezmann]] on the flanks. While on paper Saúl was seemingly out of his normal position in this role, in effect this change meant that midfielders [[Gabi (footballer, born 1983)|Gabi]] and [[Koke (footballer, born 1992)|Koke]], who were overwhelmed in the first half by Bayern's pressure, would now be shielded by Saúl's presence behind them, and the midfield would regain its much-needed composure. Eventually, the change had the desired outcome, with Atlético equalizing through Griezmann and advancing to the Champions League final.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.football-lineups.com/match/235316/|title=Bayern München vs Atletico Madrid Champions League 2015/2016|website=Football-Lineups}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|title=Atletico Madrid 2–2 Bayern Munich (agg): Atletico progress on away goals|date=5 May 2016|url=https://www.zonalmarking.net/2016/05/05/atletico-madrid-2-2-bayern-munich-atletico-progress-on-away-goals/|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160508045347/http://www.zonalmarking.net/2016/05/05/atletico-madrid-2-2-bayern-munich-atletico-progress-on-away-goals/|archive-date=8 May 2016|access-date=23 April 2021|website=www.zonalmarking.net}}</ref>

Simeone has also earned praise for instilling confidence and discipline in his players, which has helped his teams emerge victorious under adverse conditions. For example, in the last game of 2014's La Liga, at the [[Camp Nou]] against Barcelona, when Atlético needed a draw to win the championship, both [[Diego Costa]] and [[Arda Turan]] were taken out of the game due to injury in the first half, and Barcelona opened the scoring. However, Simeone was able to rally his team at half-time and Godin's header in the second half gave them the much needed draw. Simeone said: "At half-time I told them to be relaxed. In the first half we did well. I knew if we scored, it would cost them. That's what happened. There was an amazing collective effort from everyone."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.goal.com/en-gb/news/3277/la-liga/2014/05/17/4824696/simeone-im-sure-aragones-was-defending-with-us|title=Diego Simeone says sure Aragones was defending with Atletico Madrid in spirit after claiming La Liga title &#124; Goal.com|last=Willoughby|first=James|website=www.goal.com|date=17 May 2014|access-date=28 September 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180929041059/https://www.goal.com/en-gb/news/3277/la-liga/2014/05/17/4824696/simeone-im-sure-aragones-was-defending-with-us|archive-date=29 September 2018|url-status=dead}}</ref> 

During a leg in the [[2017–18 UEFA Europa League]] semi-final against [[Arsenal F.C.|Arsenal]] at the [[Emirates Stadium|Emirates]], Atlético were playing without key players Diego Costa, [[Juanfran (footballer, born 1985)|Juanfran]], [[Filipe Luís]] and [[Vitolo (footballer, born 1989)|Vitolo]], and in the 10th minute, [[Šime Vrsaljko]] was sent off with a red card, followed by Simeone being also sent off. Despite playing with ten men, without their manager at the bench, with most players in makeshift positions, and enjoying only 24% of ball possession, the team lost neither its shape nor its composure, eventually holding out to a 1–1 draw that was enough for them to progress to the final.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.football-lineups.com/match/285420/|title=Arsenal vs Atletico Madrid Europa League 2017/2018|website=Football-Lineups}}</ref> "Simeone taught us to enjoy suffering," Arda Turan said in 2015, supporting this feeling that the Argentinean coach is a man who takes the best things out of bad situations.<ref name=mirror/> Diego Godin has also said about his manager: "The players would die for him [Simeone]. He gives belief that we can compete against much bigger teams. We have great confidence in him, we are with him to the death and also he with us – and that shows on the pitch. I believe the whole team has confidence in the coach and we all know the path we have to take: he marks the way for us and we go with him until the death. That is how you achieve things."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.goal.com/en-gb/news/3310/goal-50/2016/11/09/29340332/godin-were-with-simeone-until-the-death|title=Godin: We're with Simeone until the death &#124; Goal.com|last=Boullosa|first=Adrian|website=www.goal.com|date=9 November 2016|access-date=28 September 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180929041105/https://www.goal.com/en-gb/news/3310/goal-50/2016/11/09/29340332/godin-were-with-simeone-until-the-death|archive-date=29 September 2018|url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.scoopnest.com/user/ChampionsLeague/733260615314247680-godn-34the-players-would-die-for-him-simeone-he-gives-belief-that-we-can-compete-against-much-bigger|title=Simeone : Godín players die Simeone belief compete bigger teams|website=Scoopnest}}</ref>

In the 2020–21 season, amid [[Impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on association football|COVID-19]] and injury crisis among the team's forwards, Simeone experimented with back three formations, such as 5–3–2 and 3–5–2.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.tribalfootball.com/articles/how-miracle-worker-simeone-driving-atletico-madrid-above-barcelona-real-madrid-4350871|title=How miracle worker Simeone driving Atletico Madrid above Barcelona, Real Madrid - Tribal Football|last=Keble|first=Alex|website=www.tribalfootball.com|date=10 December 2020|access-date=10 December 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201210113314/https://www.tribalfootball.com/articles/how-miracle-worker-simeone-driving-atletico-madrid-above-barcelona-real-madrid-4350871|archive-date=10 December 2020|url-status=dead}}</ref> This saw them adapt to a new, more possession-heavy style, averaging the majority of possession over the season for the first time since their victorious 2013–14 season.<ref>{{cite web|last=Askew|first=Joshua|title=Diego Simeone's new look Atlético Madrid|url=https://www.holdingmidfield.com/diego-simeone-new-look-atletico-madrid-tactics-tactical-analysis/|website=holdingmidfield.com|date=13 May 2021|publisher=Holding Midfield|access-date=18 May 2021|ref=Atleti new look tactics}}</ref>

==Personal life==

Simeone's sons [[Giovanni Simeone|Giovanni]] and [[Giuliano Simeone|Giuliano]] (from his first wife Carolina Baldini) are professional footballers, playing for [[Torino FC|Torino]] and [[Atlético Madrid|Atlético]], respectively.
His middle son [[Gianluca Simeone|Gianluca]] (from his first wife) played professionally between 2018 and 2025, in Argentina and in Spain, and, as of 2026, is now working as a football agent.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.theguardian.com/football/the-gentleman-ultra/2017/jan/24/50-best-young-footballers-italy-serie-a|title=The 50 best young footballers in Italy|work=The Guardian|last1=Newman|first1=Blair|last2=Hodges-Ramon|first2=Luca|last3=Hall|first3=Richard|last4=Ficetola|first4=Franco|last5=Neale|first5=Mark|last6=Gates|first6=Emmet|date=24 January 2017|access-date=15 February 2017}}</ref><ref name="Marca">{{cite news|title=Gianluca Simeone llega al CD Ibiza: "Tengo la garra de mi padre"|url=https://www.marca.com/futbol/mas-futbol/2020/08/15/5f330494268e3e2c698b459a.html|access-date=9 January 2019|work=Marca|date=8 January 2019}}</ref>

Simeone also has two daughters with model Carla Pereyra.<ref>{{cite news|last=Abayomi|first=Tosin|title=Atletico Madrid boss Diego Simeone welcomes baby girl with girlfriend|url=https://www.pulse.ng/story/diego-simeone-welcomes-baby-girl-valentina-with-girlfriend-carla-pereyra-2024081018334078546|access-date=24 February 2026|date=12 February 2019|newspaper=[[Pulse Nigeria]]}}</ref> Simeone and Pereyra got married in secret in June 2019.<ref>{{cite news|title=Diego Simeone and Carla Pereyra get married in secret|url=https://www.marca.com/en/football/spanish-football/2019/06/15/5d055588ca4741d24f8b45dd.html|work=Marca|access-date=31 October 2021|date=15 June 2019}}</ref>

When asked whether he believes in God, Simeone replied: “I believe in God. I believe that it’s the most important thing. It’s important to believe in something.” ''[[La Nación]]'' described him as having "an intense dialogue with God".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.mundodeportivo.com/20141030/atletico-madrid/simeone-si-el-atletico-gana-otra-liga-a-lo-mejor-nos-dan-algun-premio_54418699247.html|title=Simeone: "Si el Atlético gana otra Liga a lo mejor nos dan algún premio"|date=30 October 2014|website=Mundo Deportivo}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.lanacion.com.ar/deportes/diego-simeone-estamos-llenos-cualidades-pero-argentinos-nid2271487|title=Diego Simeone: "Estamos llenos de cualidades, pero los argentinos no sabemos jugar en equipo"|last=Grosso|first=Cristian|date=4 August 2019|website=La Nación}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.lanacion.com.ar/espectaculos/personajes/diego-simeone-y-carla-pereyra-nos-invitan-al-bautismo-de-su-hija-francesca-nid2035751|title=Diego Simeone y Carla Pereyra nos invitan al bautismo de su hija Francesca|date=21 June 2017|website=La Nación}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.hola.com/actualidad/20200219160922/carla-pereyra-simeone-bautizo-hija-hola|title=Excepcional reportaje en ¡HOLA!: el bautizo de Valentina, la hija de Carla Pereyra y Diego Simeone|date=19 February 2020|website=¡Hola!}}</ref> Simeone also attended Mass with [[Pope Leo XIV]] during his [[Visit by Pope Leo XIV to Spain|visit to Spain]] and later met with the pontiff in Madrid.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.20minutos.es/deportes/futbol/diego-simeone-se-encuentra-con-papa-leon-xiv-durante-su-visita-madrid_6980710_0.html|title=Diego Simeone se encuentra con el papa León XIV durante su visita a Madrid|date=8 June 2026|website=20Minutos}}</ref>
