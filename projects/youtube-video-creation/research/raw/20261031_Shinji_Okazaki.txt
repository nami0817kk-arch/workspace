{{nihongo|'''Shinji Okazaki'''|岡崎 慎司|Okazaki Shinji|born 16 April 1986}} is a Japanese former professional [[association football|footballer]] who played as a [[forward (association football)|forward]]. He is best known for winning the [[Premier League]] with [[Leicester City F.C.|Leicester City]] in [[2015–16 Premier League|2016]]. He is currently the manager of [[FC Basara Mainz]].

Internationally, he is [[Japan national football team|Japan]]'s third all-time top goalscorer with [[Japan national football team#Most caps and goals|50 goals]].

==Club career==
===Shimizu S-Pulse===
After graduating Takigawa Daini High School in 2004, Okazaki signed full professional terms with [[Shimizu S-Pulse]] the following year. In December 2005, he made his first [[J.League Division 1|J.League]] appearance as a substitute against [[Sanfrecce Hiroshima]]. On 15 April 2007, he scored his first professional goal against [[Kawasaki Frontale]]. Okazaki ended his [[2007 J.League Division 1|first season]] as a first team player with three goals from 13 matches.

Okazaki scored ten times in 27 matches during the [[2008 J. League Division 1|2008 J. League season]] and also represented S-Pulse in the [[2008 J. League Cup#Final|J. League Cup Final]], where the team lost 2–0 to [[Oita Trinita]]. He continued his promising goalscoring in the [[2009 J. League Division 1|2009]] and [[2010 J. League Division 1|2010 season]]s and, in January 2011, was signed by German side [[VfB Stuttgart]] of the [[Bundesliga]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.vfb.de/en/aktuell/meldungen/2010/verpflichtung-shinji-okazaki/page/439-0-1-1296449195.html|title=Shinji Okazaki in Stuttgart|work=VfB Stuttgart|access-date=30 January 2011|date=30 January 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120304203717/http://www.vfb.de/en/aktuell/meldungen/2010/verpflichtung-shinji-okazaki/page/439-0-1-1296449195.html|archive-date=4 March 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref>

===VfB Stuttgart===
[[File:Shinji okazaki.jpg|thumb|left|upright|Okazaki with [[VfB Stuttgart]] in 2011]]
On 30 January 2011, Okazaki signed a three-and-a-half-year contract with Stuttgart.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.vfb.de/en/aktuell/meldungen/2010/pk-shinji-okazaki/page/446-0-1-1296469536.html|title=Shinji Okazaki signs for VfB|publisher=VfB Stuttgart|access-date=30 January 2011|date=30 January 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120304203728/http://www.vfb.de/en/aktuell/meldungen/2010/pk-shinji-okazaki/page/446-0-1-1296469536.html|archive-date=4 March 2012|url-status=dead}}</ref> On 17 February 2011, clearance to play for the club was granted for Okazaki by [[FIFA]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.vfb.de/en/aktuell/meldungen/2010/freigabe-okazaki/page/486-0-1-1297962975.html|title=Clearance for Shinji Okazaki|publisher=VfB Stuttgart|access-date=17 February 2011|date=17 February 2011|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110719112218/http://www.vfb.de/en/aktuell/meldungen/2010/freigabe-okazaki/page/486-0-1-1297962975.html|archive-date=19 July 2011}}</ref> He made his competitive debut for VfB on the same night in a [[UEFA Europa League|Europa League]] match against [[S.L. Benfica|Benfica]]. On 20 February 2011, Okazaki made his [[Bundesliga]] debut for VfB against [[Bayer 04 Leverkusen]]. Okazaki scored his first goal against [[Hannover 96]], which he scored from outside the box.<ref>{{cite web | url = http://search.japantimes.co.jp/cgi-bin/sc20110509a2.html | title = Okazaki gets maiden Stuttgart goal | date = 9 May 2011 | access-date = 25 September 2011 | work = The Japan Times}}</ref>

Okazaki came on as a second-half substitute for Stuttgart in their first game of the [[2011–12 Bundesliga]] season against [[FC Schalke 04|Schalke 04]]. His side were up 2–0 and Okazaki added a goal scoring from long range in the 90th minute, sealing a 3–0 win.<ref>{{cite web | url = http://soccernet.espn.go.com/gamecast?id=321039&cc=5901 | title = VfB Stuttgart 3 – 0 Schalke 04 | publisher = ESPN Soccernet | date = 6 August 2011 | access-date = 25 September 2011 | archive-date = 26 October 2012 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20121026130946/http://soccernet.espn.go.com/gamecast?id=321039&cc=5901 | url-status = dead }}</ref> Okazaki's goal from a bicycle kick on 19 February 2012 in a Bundesliga match of VfB Stuttgart against Hannover 96 was selected [[Goal of the Month (Germany)|Goal of the Month]]. After [[Yasuhiko Okudera]] in April 1978, Okazaki was the second player from Japan to win this award in Germany.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.vfb.de/en/aktuell/meldungen/news/2012/tor-des-monats-okazaki/page/1354-0-1-1331528931.html|title=Goal of the Month|publisher=VfB Stuttgart|access-date=12 March 2012|date=11 March 2012|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120312105945/http://www.vfb.de/en/aktuell/meldungen/news/2012/tor-des-monats-okazaki/page/1354-0-1-1331528931.html|archive-date=12 March 2012}}</ref>

Okazaki had a poor [[2012–13 VfB Stuttgart season|2012–13]] season, scoring only once in the [[2012–13 Bundesliga|Bundesliga]]. However, he scored his first goals in European competition with two goals against [[FC Steaua București|Steaua București]] in the [[2012–13 UEFA Europa League|Europa League]].

===Mainz 05===
On 1 July 2013, Okazaki moved to [[1. FSV Mainz 05|Mainz 05]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.vfb.de/en/aktuell/meldungen/news/2013/wechsel-shinji-okazaki-vfb-stuttgart/page/2370-1-1-.html|title=Shinji Okazaki moves to Mainz|work=VfB Stuttgart|access-date=25 June 2013|date=25 June 2013|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141023144626/http://www.vfb.de/en/aktuell/meldungen/news/2013/wechsel-shinji-okazaki-vfb-stuttgart/page/2370-1-1-.html|archive-date=23 October 2014}}</ref> He scored his first goal for Mainz on his debut in a 3–2 win against his former club Stuttgart on the opening matchday of the [[2013–14 Bundesliga]] season.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.bundesliga.com/en/liga/news/2013/0000264412.php|title=Okazaki and Müller fire Mainz to victory|date=11 August 2013|access-date=13 October 2013|work=Bundesliga|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131022152122/http://www.bundesliga.com/en/liga/news/2013/0000264412.php|archive-date=22 October 2013}}</ref> At the end of the season he ended with 15 league goals.

Okazaki became the most prolific Japanese player in Bundesliga history on 13 September 2014, scoring his 27th and 28th league goals in Mainz's 3–1 win over [[Hertha BSC|Hertha Berlin]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.espnfc.com/german-bundesliga/match/396972/hertha-berlin-mainz/report |title=Hertha Berlin 1–3 Mainz |work=ESPNFC |date=13 September 2014 |access-date=13 September 2014 }}</ref>

===Leicester City===
====2015–16====
[[File:Shinji Okazaki.png|thumb|Okazaki (middle) playing for [[Leicester City F.C.|Leicester City]] in 2016]]
On 26 June 2015, Okazaki moved to [[Premier League]] side [[Leicester City F.C.|Leicester City]] for a fee believed to be in the region of £7&nbsp;million.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.lcfc.com/news/article/shinji-okazaki-agrees-leicester-city-deal-2509148.aspx|title=Shinji Okazaki Agrees Leicester City Deal|work=Leicester City|access-date=26 June 2015|date=26 June 2015}}</ref>

He made his debut for the club on 8 August 2015 in Leicester City's 4–2 opening day win against [[Sunderland A.F.C.|Sunderland]], and scored his first goal for the club the following week in a 2–1 win against [[West Ham United F.C.|West Ham United]]. On 19 December 2015, Okazaki scored the winner in a 3–2 win over [[Everton F.C.|Everton]] at [[Goodison Park]], to ensure that Leicester City topped the table at Christmas.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.hitc.com/en-gb/2015/12/19/tt-48-touches-3-shots-leicester-city-star-shinji-okazaki-superb|title=Shinji Okazaki Stars in Everton Win|access-date=20 December 2015|date=19 December 2015}}</ref>

On 10 January 2016, Okazaki scored Leicester's second goal in the [[FA Cup]] clash at [[White Hart Lane]] with [[Tottenham Hotspur F.C.|Tottenham Hotspur]] despite drawing to a [[Harry Kane]] penalty in the 90th minute. On 14 March 2016, Okazaki scored in a league fixture against [[Newcastle United F.C.|Newcastle United]]: a spectacular overhead strike that won Leicester City the game 1–0 and took them five points clear at the top of the Premier League table.<ref>{{cite news |date=14 March 2016 |title=Leicester City 1-0 Newcastle United |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/football/35741942 |access-date=27 May 2016 |work=[[BBC Sport]] |language=en-GB}}</ref> Leicester City went on to win the league that year, making Okazaki the second Japanese player to win the Premier League after [[Shinji Kagawa]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.japantimes.co.jp/sports/2016/05/04/soccer/okazaki-in-wonderland-after-leicesters-premier-league-triumph|title=Okazaki in wonderland after Leicester's Premier League triumph|work=The Japan Times|access-date=27 May 2016|date=4 May 2016}}</ref>

His performances in the 2015–16 season earned him the [[Asian Footballer of the Year|Asian International Player of the Year]] award in December 2016.<ref name="Okazaki: I’m thrilled to win Asian award">{{citation|title = Okazaki: I'm thrilled to win Asian award|url = https://www.fifa.com/worldcup/news/y=2016/m=12/news=okazaki-i-m-thrilled-to-win-asian-award-2859619.html|archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20170403192957/http://www.fifa.com/worldcup/news/y=2016/m=12/news=okazaki-i-m-thrilled-to-win-asian-award-2859619.html|url-status = dead|archive-date = 3 April 2017|publisher = FIFA|date = 12 December 2016|access-date = 1 January 2017}}</ref>

====2016–17====
Okazaki scored twice in a 2–4 defeat to Chelsea in the third round of the 2016–17 [[EFL Cup]] on 20 September 2016.<ref>{{cite news|last1=Rose|first1=Gary|title=Leicester City 2–4 Chelsea|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/37346049|work=BBC Sport|access-date=20 September 2016}}</ref> He scored his first league goal of the season in a 3–1 win over [[Crystal Palace F.C.|Crystal Palace]] on 22 October 2016, earning him [[Man of the match]] and achieving an 84.8% pass success rate, more than any other Leicester player.<ref name="Leicester City 3-1 Crystal Palace">{{citation|title = Leicester City 3–1 Crystal Palace|url = https://www.bbc.com/sport/football/37663854|publisher = BBC Sport|author = Aimee Lewis|date = 22 October 2016|access-date = 9 January 2017}}</ref> On 22 November 2016, Okazaki scored in a 2–1 win over [[Club Brugge KV|Club Brugge]] in the [[2016–17 UEFA Champions League group stage|Champions League group stage]], becoming the sixth Japanese player to score a [[UEFA Champions League|Champions League]] goal and the first since [[Keisuke Honda]] in November 2013.<ref name="Leicester City 2-1 Club Brugge">{{citation|title = Leicester City 2–1 Club Brugge|url = https://www.bbc.com/sport/football/38054997|publisher = BBC Sport|author = Neil Johnston|date = 22 November 2016|access-date = 14 October 2017}}</ref> He finished the season with only three league goals.<ref name="Shinji Okazaki reveals secrets to Leicester City goal-scoring form">{{citation|title = Shinji Okazaki reveals secrets to Leicester City goal-scoring form|url = http://www.leicestermercury.co.uk/sport/football/football-news/shinji-okazaki-reveals-secrets-leicester-508090|work = Leicester Mercury|author = James Sharpe|date = 20 September 2017|access-date = 25 September 2017}}</ref>

====2017–2019====
Okazaki was in goalscoring form at the start to the [[2017–18 Leicester City F.C. season|2017–18 season]], scoring four goals in six appearances for Leicester in all competitions.<ref>{{citation|title = Leicester City 2–3 Liverpool Verdict: Jamie Vardy misses penalty in another frustrating defeat in five-goal thriller|url = http://www.leicestermercury.co.uk/sport/football/match-reports/leicester-city-2-3-liverpool-522926|work = Leicester Mercury|author = Rob Tanner|date = 23 September 2017|access-date = 25 September 2017}}</ref> He was released by Leicester at the end of the 2018–19 season.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/48205542|title=Leicester's Christian Fuchs signs new deal as Danny Simpson & Shinji Okazaki leave|date=8 May 2019|access-date=9 May 2019|publisher=BBC Sport}}</ref>

===Málaga===
On 30 July 2019, Okazaki signed a one-year contract with [[Málaga CF]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://football-espana.net/79511/malaga-sign-former-leicester-striker-shinzi-okazaki|title=Malaga sign former Leicester striker Shinzi Okazaki|date=30 July 2019|publisher=Football Espana}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.foxsportsasia.com/football/transfer-news/1148733/okazaki-signs-one-year-deal-at-malaga/|title=Okazaki signs one-year deal at Malaga|date=30 July 2019|publisher=Fox Sports Asia|access-date=30 July 2019|archive-date=30 July 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190730161249/https://www.foxsportsasia.com/football/transfer-news/1148733/okazaki-signs-one-year-deal-at-malaga/|url-status=dead}}</ref> However, Málaga canceled its contract with Okazaki after just 34 days, since his wage demands exceeded its maximum budget as permitted by the league.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.football-espana.net/80880/shinji-okazaki-leaves-malaga-without-playing/|title=Shinji Okazaki leaves Malaga without playing|date=3 September 2019|publisher=Football Espana}}</ref>

===Huesca===
On 4 September 2019, the [[free agent]] Okazaki signed a one-year contract with [[Segunda División]] side [[SD Huesca|Huesca]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.sdhuesca.es/noticia/okazaki-la-guinda-al-ataque-de-la-sd-huesca|title=Okazaki, la guinda al ataque de la SD Huesca|trans-title=Okazaki, the cherry on SD Huesca's attack|language=es|date=4 September 2019|access-date=4 September 2019|website=SD Huesca's official website}}</ref> On 8 September 2019, he made his debut for Huesca as a substitute.

On 25 July 2020, after scoring 12 times as his side [[2019–20 Segunda División|achieved promotion]] to [[La Liga]], Okazaki renewed his contract for a further year.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.sdhuesca.es/noticia/shinji-okazaki-renueva-con-la-sd-huesca-hasta-2021|title=Shinji Okazaki renueva con la SD Huesca hasta 2021|trans-title=Shinji Okazaki renews with SD Huesca until 2021|publisher=SD Huesca|language=es|date=25 July 2020|accessdate=31 August 2021}}</ref>

===Cartagena===
On 31 August 2021, Okazaki moved to [[FC Cartagena]] in the second division on a one-year contract.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.fccartagena.es/noticia/okazaki-experiencia-y-gol-para-la-delantera-del-fc-cartagena|title=Okazaki, experiencia y gol para la delantera del FC Cartagena|trans-title=Okazaki, experience and goals for the forward lines of FC Cartagena|publisher=FC Cartagena|language=es|date=31 August 2021|accessdate=31 August 2021}}</ref>

===Sint-Truiden===
On 19 August 2022, Okazaki signed with [[Sint-Truidense V.V.|Sint-Truiden]] in Belgium. On 26 February 2024, he announced he would retire from playing at the end of the 2023–24 season.<ref>{{cite news |title=Shinji Okazaki: Ex-Leicester forward and Premier League title winner announces retirement |url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/football/68400502 |access-date=26 February 2024 |work=BBC Sport |date=26 February 2024}}</ref>

==International career==
[[File:Shinji Okazaki - International friendly match (1) cropped.jpg|thumb|upright|left|Okazaki with [[Japan national football team|Japan]] in 2009]]
After competing for the country's [[Japan national under-23 football team|under-23]] team at the [[Football at the 2008 Summer Olympics – Men's tournament|2008 Olympic Games]] in China, Okazaki made his full international debut for the [[Japan national football team|Japan senior national team]] against the [[United Arab Emirates national football team|United Arab Emirates]] in October 2008.<ref name=nationalteam>{{cite web| url =http://www.japannationalfootballteam.com/en/players/okazaki_shinji.html| title =OKAZAKI Shinji| publisher =Japan National Football Team Database| url-status =dead| archive-url =https://web.archive.org/web/20130922094423/http://www.japannationalfootballteam.com/en/players/okazaki_shinji.html| archive-date =22 September 2013}}</ref> On 20 January 2009, he scored his first goal for ''Samurai Blue'' in the team's opening [[2011 AFC Asian Cup]] [[2011 AFC Asian Cup qualification|qualifier]] against [[Yemen national football team|Yemen]].<ref name=nationalteam/>

In October 2009, Okazaki scored [[hat-trick]]s in consecutive matches as Japan beat [[Hong Kong national football team|Hong Kong]] and [[Togo national football team|Togo]] 6–0 and 5–0 respectively.<ref name=nationalteam/> He was named The World's Top Goal Scorer of 2009 by the [[International Federation of Football History & Statistics|IFFHS]] for his 15 goals with the national team.<ref>{{cite web |date=9 January 2010 |title=La IFFHS designa al japonés Shinji Okazaki mejor goleador de 2009 |url=http://www.as.com/futbol/articulo/iffhs-designa-japones-shinji-okazaki/20100109dasdasftb_2/Tes |access-date=5 January 2013 |publisher=[[Diario AS]] |language=es}}</ref>

Okazaki was included in the [[Japan national football team|2010 FIFA World Cup squads]] for the [[2010 FIFA World Cup]] and appeared as a substitute in all four of Japan's matches. He scored once in the final [[2010 FIFA World Cup Group E|Group E]] match, a 3–1 defeat of [[Denmark national football team|Denmark]], to send Japan into the second round.<ref name=nationalteam/>

On 8 October 2010, Okazaki scored as Japan beat [[Argentina national football team|Argentina]] 1–0 in friendly match to record its first ever win over the [[1978 FIFA World Cup|1978]] and [[1986 FIFA World Cup|1986]] world champions.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://news.smh.com.au/breaking-news-sport/japan-beat-argentina-for-1st-time-20101009-16chw.html|title=Japan beat Argentina for 1st time|date=9 October 2010|access-date=13 October 2013|work=The Sydney Morning Herald}}</ref>

Okazaki scored his third international hat-trick in a 5–0 [[2011 AFC Asian Cup Group B|Group B]] win against [[Saudi Arabia national football team|Saudi Arabia]] at the [[2011 AFC Asian Cup]]. He then scored his kick in the semi-final [[Penalty shootout|shootout]] win over [[South Korea national football team|South Korea]] and played all 120 minutes of the [[2011 AFC Asian Cup Final|final]] as Japan beat [[Australia men's national soccer team|Australia]] to win the trophy for a fourth time.<ref name="nationalteam" />

Okazaki finished as [[2014 FIFA World Cup qualification (AFC)#Goalscorers|top goalscorer]] in the [[2014 FIFA World Cup qualification (AFC)|Asian section]] of [[2014 FIFA World Cup qualification|qualification]] for the [[2014 FIFA World Cup]] with eight goals.

Okazaki scored twice at the [[2013 FIFA Confederations Cup]], against [[Italy national football team|Italy]] and [[Mexico national football team|Mexico]] respectively. However, Japan lost all three of its matches and was eliminated at the end of the group stage.<ref>{{cite web|url= https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/22916862|title= Japan 1 Mexico 2|date=22 June 2013|work=BBC Sport|access-date=24 June 2013 }}</ref>
[[File:Japan national football team World Cup 2018.jpg|thumb|Okazaki (front row, far right) with Japan at the [[2018 FIFA World Cup]]]]
In June 2014, Okazaki was selected in the Japan's squad for the 2014 FIFA World Cup.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/sport/football/teams/japan/10826657/Japan-World-Cup-2014-squad.html |archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/archive/20220112/https://www.telegraph.co.uk/sport/football/teams/japan/10826657/Japan-World-Cup-2014-squad.html |archive-date=12 January 2022 |url-access=subscription |url-status=live|title=Japan World Cup 2014 squad|date=2 June 2014|access-date=26 June 2014|work=The Telegraph}}{{cbignore}}</ref> In the team's third group match, a 4–1 defeat to [[Colombia national football team|Colombia]], he scored Samurai Blue's only goal with a header in the 45th minute.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/0/football/25455848|title=Japan 1–4 Colombia|date=24 June 2014|access-date=26 June 2014|publisher=BBC}}</ref>

Okazaki was included in Japan's squad for the [[2015 AFC Asian Cup]] and scored in a 4–0 defeat of [[Palestine national football team|Palestine]] during the group stage.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.usatoday.com/story/sports/soccer/2015/01/12/japan-opens-asian-cup-with-4-0-win-over-palestine-in-group-d/21621275/|title=Japan opens Asian Cup with 4-0 win over Palestine in Group D|date=12 January 2015|work=[[USA Today]]}}</ref>

In March 2016, Okazaki was presented with a commemorative shirt with 100 on the back in a presentation to celebrate his 100th international appearance for Japan.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.jfa.jp/eng/national_team_2016/news/00009301/|title=SAMURAI BLUE beat Syria 5–0 to top group in FIFA World Cup Russia Asian Qualifiers Round 2's last match |website=JFA｜公益財団法人日本サッカー協会|access-date=22 April 2023}}</ref>

Okazaki last played for the national team in a [[2019 Copa América Group C#Ecuador vs Japan|1-1 draw]] against [[Ecuador national football team|Ecuador]] at the [[2019 Copa América]]. Okazaki ended his career as Japan's third all-time goalscorer with [[Japan national football team#Records|50 international goals]].<ref>{{cite web | website=FIFA | url=https://www.fifa.com/fifaplus/en/articles/shinji-okazaki-life-in-spain-japan-leicester-city-cartagena-2022-world-cup | access-date=22 April 2023|title=Shinji Okazaki life in Spain}}</ref>

==Managerial career==
Okazaki co-founded the German football club Basara Mainz in the 2014–15 season when he was a player of [[Mainz 05]].<ref>{{cite news|title=Shinji Okazaki: "Ich sehe mich nicht als Superstar"|date=October 2014|last=Schächter|first= Tobias |work=bundesliga.com|url=https://www.bundesliga.com/de/bundesliga/news/shinji-okazaki-ich-sehe-mich-nicht-als-superstar-interview-1-fsv-mainz-05.jsp }}</ref> Now he serves as the club adviser. The club is run by the other co-founder, Takashi Yamashita, former player of Mainz's reserve team [[1.FSV Mainz 05 II|Mainz 05 II]]. Basara Mainz started in tier 11 of the [[German football league system]].
