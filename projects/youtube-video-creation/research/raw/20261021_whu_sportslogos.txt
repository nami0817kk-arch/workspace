West Ham United Launches Fan Consultation on Possible Changes to Club Crest
Tuesday, June 30, 2026
Tuesday, June 30, 2026
Glenn Cook
One of 
England
‘s most famous soccer teams is thinking about changing up its look, and leadership is looking to their fans for more input.
West Ham United
 — the east London club that has played regularly in 
England’s top flight
 since its founding in 1895, but was relegated to the 
EFL Championship
 for 2026-27 — announced on Monday, June 29, that it’s launching a consultation process with its supporters to determine the future direction of the club’s official crest.
On 
the anniversary of the formation of Thames Ironworks FC in 1895
 the Club can confirm that a full and comprehensive supporter-led process will soon begin to ensure its world-famous crest best reflects the Club’s unique heritage, identity and culture.
— 
West Ham United
We are pleased to inform supporters of the launch of a new fan consultation on the future direction of the Club’s official crest.
For more details ⬇️
— West Ham United (@WestHam) 
June 29, 2026
This consultation will take place throughout the 2026-27 season, and any changes that come out of it will take effect at the start of the 2028-29 season “to allow appropriate time for manufacturing and production deadlines.” The process will begin with West Ham’s Fan Advisory Board (FAB), and if their feedback is positive, it will continue with season ticket holders, Claret members and supporters who attended more than five home matches in 2025-26.
West Ham’s current crest was adopted in 2016 when the club moved into London Stadium, which was originally built for the 2012 Summer 
Olympic Games
. It features gold rivet hammers — tools used in the shipbuilding industry and a mainstay element of crests since changing its name to West Ham United in 1900 — inside a claret shield with a light blue outline. The club name is spelled out in white at the top, and “LONDON” sits at the bottom.
Courtesy 
Wikipedia
Starting in the 1950s, other versions of the crest also included a depiction of 
Anne Boleyn’s Tower
, which stood next to West Ham’s old home stadium, the 
Boleyn Ground
. The tower was demolished in 1955, and the Boleyn Ground was demolished in 2016 after the club moved out.
Courtesy 
West Ham United
“With 2026 marking ten years since our current crest was launched, now is the right time for us to review its future. The Board and I want to ensure that the Club’s history and heritage are appropriately reflected in everything we do, and that our loyal fans should play a leading role in determining how that is delivered. To that end, we will ensure any change is supporter-led and we will be working with the FAB as an independent body every step of the way. I look forward to working with them and the wider supporter base as we embark on this exciting project.”
— Karim Virani, interim CEO, West Ham United
West Ham said in its announcement that it would provide regular updates throughout the consultation process. It has also set up 
an FAQ page on its website
 for the consultation.
