[[Arsenal F.C.|Arsenal Football Club]] is an English professional [[association football]] club based in [[Holloway, London]]. The club was formed in [[Woolwich]] in 1886 as Dial Square before it was shortly renamed to Royal Arsenal, and then Woolwich Arsenal in 1893.{{sfnp|Soar|Tyler|2011|p=24}} They became the first southern member{{efn|A club located in the southern [[counties]] of England. Initially these were amateur clubs, as professionalism in football was not as readily accepted in the south as in the north. In the 1893–94 season, Arsenal (under its former name Woolwich Arsenal) turned professional and became the first southern club admitted to the northern-oriented [[The Football League|Football League]]. The following year saw the creation of the [[Southern Football League]], which was composed of amateur and professional teams. By the [[1920–21 in English football|1920–21]] season, the top division of the Southern Football League was absorbed by the Football League, to create its third division.<ref>{{cite book |first=Alan |last=Tomlinson |title=A Dictionary of Sports Studies |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=r_Ujf9-TmBUC&pg=PA196 |page=196 |publisher=Oxford University Press |year=2010 |isbn=978-0-19-921381-8 }}</ref><ref>{{cite book |title=1895: Drama, Disaster and Disgrace in Late Victorian Britain |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=vYhvAAAAQBAJ&pg=PT39 |page=39 |first=Nicholas |last=Freeman |publisher=Edinburgh University Press |year=2011 |isbn=978-0-7486-4056-0 }}</ref>|group=nb}} admitted into [[the Football League]] in 1893, having spent their first four seasons solely participating in cup tournaments and [[Exhibition game#Association football|friendlies]].{{sfnp|Joy|1952|p=9}} The club's name was shortened to Arsenal in 1914, a year after moving to [[Arsenal Stadium|Highbury]].{{sfnp|Joy|1952|p=32}} In spite of finishing fifth in the [[Football League Second Division|Second Division]] in 1915, Arsenal rejoined the [[Football League First Division|First Division]] at the expense of local rivals [[Tottenham Hotspur F.C.|Tottenham Hotspur]] when football resumed after the First World War.{{sfnp|Joy|1952|p=28}} Since that time, they have not fallen below the first tier of the [[English football league system]] and hold the record for the longest uninterrupted time in the top flight.<ref name="RSSSF_Div_Movements">{{cite web|last1=Ross|first1=James|last2=Heneghan|first2=Michael|last3=Orford|first3=Stuart|last4=Culliton|first4=Eoin|title=English Clubs Divisional Movements 1888-2016|url=https://www.rsssf.org/tablese/engall.html |publisher=[[Rec.Sport.Soccer Statistics Foundation]]|access-date=5 August 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160805092856/http://www.rsssf.com/tablese/engall.html|archive-date=5 August 2016|date=23 June 2016|url-status=live}}</ref>

In the 1930s, Arsenal were the dominant side of England, winning five [[List of English football champions|league championship]]s and two [[FA Cup]]s. Their fortunes waned, but the club soon enjoyed infrequent periods of success, including [[Inter-Cities Fairs Cup]] triumph and a first league and cup [[Double (association football)#England|double]] in the 1970s. During the late 1980s, Arsenal had built a side that threatened [[Liverpool F.C.|Liverpool]]'s league dominance, and performed greatly in cup competitions. The club played an active role in the formation of the [[Premier League]] in 1992, won the FA Cup in 1993 and the [[European Cup Winners Cup]] in 1994 and two doubles followed in 1998 and 2002. Arsenal made league history in [[2003–04 in English football|2003–04]] when they became the first team in a 38-game season to go unbeaten.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.avfc.co.uk/News/2009/12/26/match-pack-arsenal-v-villa |title=Match Pack: Arsenal v Villa |publisher=Aston Villa F.C |date=26 December 2009 |access-date=25 July 2016 |archive-date=21 August 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160821164150/https://www.avfc.co.uk/News/2009/12/26/match-pack-arsenal-v-villa |url-status=live }}</ref> In the 2000s, Arsenal were finalists in both the [[UEFA Cup]] and [[UEFA Champions League]],<ref name="stattocomplete">{{cite web |url=http://www.statto.com/football/teams/arsenal/history/cup-finals |title=Arsenal – Complete cup finals |publisher=Statto Organisation |access-date=26 July 2016 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160304082911/http://www.statto.com/football/teams/arsenal/history/cup-finals |archive-date=4 March 2016}}</ref> and have since equalled [[Real Madrid C.F.|Real Madrid]]'s record for most consecutive seasons in the latter competition.<ref>{{cite news |first=Mandeep |last=Sanghera |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/34203643 |title=Dinamo Zagreb 2–1 Arsenal |publisher=[[BBC Sport]] |date=16 September 2015 |access-date=26 July 2016 |archive-date=19 September 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150919185408/http://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/0/football/34203643 |url-status=live }}</ref>

As of the end of the [[2025-26 in English football|2025–26]] season, the club's first team have spent 109 seasons in the top division of English football, and 13 in the second. Their worst league finish to date is 10th in the second tier, their placing at the end of the [[1896–97 in English football|1896–97]] season. Arsenal's best-ever start to a Premier League season came in [[2022–23 in English football|2022–23]], when they won nine of their first ten matches.<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/articles/cz91wq5e42ro |title=S outhampton v Arsenal: Head-to-head stats |publisher=BBC Sport |date=21 October 2022 |access-date=2 January 2023 |archive-date=2 January 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230102135100/https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/articles/cz91wq5e42ro |url-status=live }}</ref> The club's longest period without a competitive honour is 17 years, between the [[1953–54 in English football|1953–54]] and [[1969–70 in English football|1969–70]] seasons. [[Ted Drake]] holds the record for most competitive goals in a single season for Arsenal; he scored 44 during the [[1934–35 in English football|1934–35]] campaign. ==History==
