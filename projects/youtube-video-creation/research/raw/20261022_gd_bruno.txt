Prefer the Guardian on GoogleMichael Carrick’s future as Manchester United manager is in the balance after the club’s humiliating 4-1 Champions League defeat away against Como on Wednesday.
Before Bournemouth’s visit to Old Trafford on Sunday his future may be discussed by Jason Wilcox, the director of football, Omar Berrada, the chief executive, and Sir Jim Ratcliffe, the co-owner who controls sporting policy.
Read moreOn a torrid night, an “angry” Carrick insisted afterwards he had not given his position any thought but was forced to defend himself against his own captain, Bruno Fernandes, who questioned the manager’s tactics of not pressing against Cesc Fàbregas’s team. Carrick in turn criticised his side for not running. The defeat at the Stadio Giuseppe Sinigaglia leaves United with only two wins all season from 11 games. They are 13th in the Premier League and were eliminated from the Carabao Cup last month.
Carrick was asked if he fears for his job. “Listen, I’ve not given my own position any thought,” he said. “[My thoughts are] for the supporters – I was one of them watching that and I wasn’t happy at all.”
He was then asked if he could walk away following the latest defeat in Europe. “No. It’s not about me right now and my feelings in that regard. It’s more of as a team and as a football club, what we want to look like, what we should look like. And as I said, the results can come and go at times, but you got to do it in a certain way. And that wasn’t it.”
Seven hundred United fans travelled, but by the end only around 200 remained and Carrick went to apologise to them, noticeably ignoring his players. After United conceded Nico Paz’s second-minute goal, they were unable to mount any resistance. By 57 minutes Como led 3-0, due to Tasos Douvikas’s strike and Paz’s second, before Jacobo Ramón completed the scoring for the Italian side on 76 minutes. Joshua Zirkzee scored in added time for the visitors.

Photograph: Alessandro Garofalo/ReutersFernandes openly challenged Carrick’s strategy during the match. “It’s a decision from the staff to not press man-to-man and sometimes leave space behind and try to get the ball, wait for them, but sometimes when you give space to these teams they get confidence,” he said.
The captain was asked if Carrick’s tactics were wrong. “That’s on the manager and staff to see, not me,” he said. “Sometimes you have to be more brave under pressure, take the risk of pressing high and making them uncomfortable. As you saw with them, the centre-back was coming to mark me in my own half. We need to do that as a team but we were unable to.”

Fernandes’s criticism was put to his manager after the match. “We actually half-pressed man-to-man in the second half and then conceded a goal from that,” Carrick said. “Whatever system you play, whatever tactics you play, if you don’t run, and you don’t run forward, and you don’t run back, you ain’t going to win.”
Carrick was asked how he can raise the mood before Bournemouth’s visit. “I’m bitterly disappointed, I’m frustrated; more than frustrated, I’m really angry. But you’ve got to move on,” he said. “You got to deal with what’s next and see what that looks like.
“At this moment in time, there’s a lot to do before that next game to get the team ready because that was a difficult night for everyone. I have to apologise to the supporters.”
