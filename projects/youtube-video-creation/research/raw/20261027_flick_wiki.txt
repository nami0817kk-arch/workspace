'''Hans-Dieter''' "'''Hansi'''" '''Flick''' ({{IPA|de|ˈhanzi ˈflɪk|lang}};<ref>{{Cite web |date=7 November 2020 |title=Ein Jahr Flick – sein Aufstieg zum Rekord-König {{!}} SPORT1 |trans-title=One year of Flick – his rise to record king {{!}} SPORT1 |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nNN_xVLSJjQ |access-date=6 May 2025 |website=[[YouTube]] |publisher=[[Sport1 (Germany)|SPORT1]] |language=german}}</ref> born 24 February 1965) is a German professional [[Manager (association football)|football manager]] and former [[Football player|player]] who is the head coach of [[La Liga]] club [[FC Barcelona|Barcelona]]. He is widely considered to be one of the best managers in the world.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Karlsen |first1=Tor-Kristian |title=Ranking the top 30 best men's club managers in Europe |url=https://www.espn.com/soccer/story/_/id/45796723/ranking-top-30-best-mens-club-managers-europe |website=[[ESPN.com]] |access-date=2025-09-26 |language=en |date=2025-08-12}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last1=White |first1=Mark |last2=Mewis |first2=Joe |last3=Stronge |first3=Isaac Stacey |last4=Nee |first4=Chris |last5=Holt |first5=Matthew |title=Ranked! The 50 best managers in the world right now |url=https://www.fourfourtwo.com/features/best-managers-in-the-world-right-now/2 |website=[[FourFourTwo]] |access-date=2026-02-13 |language=en |date=2025-06-25}}</ref>

A [[midfielder]], Flick played for [[SV Sandhausen]], [[FC Bayern Munich|Bayern Munich]], and [[1. FC Köln]] during his career as a player. At Bayern, he won four [[Bundesliga]] titles as well as one [[DFB-Pokal]] title. After playing at 1. FC Köln, Flick began his managerial career as player-manager of fourth-division club [[Victoria Bammental]]. In 2000, he became manager of fellow fourth-division side [[TSG 1899 Hoffenheim]], leading the team to promotion to the [[Regionalliga Süd (1994–2012)|Regionalliga Süd]] before leaving in 2005. From 2006 to 2014, Flick was the assistant coach of the German national team under [[Joachim Löw]], contributing to their victory at the [[2014 FIFA World Cup]]. He subsequently served as the sporting director of the [[German Football Association]] (DFB) from 2014 to 2017.

Having rejoined Bayern Munich as an assistant coach in 2019, Flick was made interim manager following the departure of [[Niko Kovač]] in November 2019. He was later appointed permanently, and won the [[2019–20 UEFA Champions League|UEFA Champions League]] that season, completing the club's second [[Treble (association football)|continental treble]]. In 2021, Flick also led the side to a [[2020 FIFA Club World Cup|FIFA Club World Cup]] and another [[Bundesliga]] title.<ref name="Germany head coach">{{cite news |url=https://www.dfb.de/news/detail/hansi-flick-wird-neuer-bundestrainer-226926/ |title=Hansi Flick wird neuer Bundestrainer |trans-title=Hansi Flick is the new national coach |work=[[German Football Association]] |date=25 May 2021 |access-date=25 May 2021 |language=de |archive-date=25 May 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210525090813/https://www.dfb.de/news/detail/hansi-flick-wird-neuer-bundestrainer-226926/ |url-status=live }}</ref> He, along with [[Pep Guardiola]] and [[Luis Enrique]], are the only managers to have ever achieved a [[Sextuple (football)|sextuple]] with their team. He later replaced Löw in charge of the Germany national team in 2021, leading the team to qualification for the [[2022 FIFA World Cup]], before being dismissed in 2023. In 2024, he joined Spanish club Barcelona, winning the domestic treble ([[Supercopa de España]], [[Copa del Rey]] and [[La Liga]]) in his debut season, then winning La Liga and the Supercopa de España in his next.

==Playing career==
During his playing career, he was a midfielder who played 104 matches for [[FC Bayern Munich|Bayern Munich]] and scored five goals between 1985 and 1990.<ref name=":1">{{cite web |url=https://www.kicker.de/hans-dieter-flick-10757/spieler/1-bundesliga/2010-11/1-fc-koeln-16 |title=Flick, Hans-Dieter |website=[[Kicker (magazine)|Kicker]] |publisher=[[Kicker (sports magazine)|kicker]] |location=Nuremberg |language=de |access-date=26 May 2011 |archive-date=28 October 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191028174253/https://www.kicker.de/hans-dieter-flick-10757/spieler/1-bundesliga/2010-11/1-fc-koeln-16 }}</ref> At Bayern, he won four [[Bundesliga]] titles as well as one [[DFB-Pokal]] title, and played in the [[1987 European Cup Final]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.bundesliga.com/en/bundesliga/news/hansi-flick-10-things-on-bayern-munich-s-record-breaking-title-winning-coach-11663 |title=Hansi Flick: 10 things on Bayern Munich's record-breaking, treble-winning coach |website=Bundesliga |date=August 2020 |access-date=25 May 2021 |archive-date=25 May 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210525110929/https://www.bundesliga.com/en/bundesliga/news/hansi-flick-10-things-on-bayern-munich-s-record-breaking-title-winning-coach-11663 |url-status=live }}</ref> He later played 44 matches for [[1. FC Köln|Köln]] before retiring from professional football in 1993 due to injuries. His last spell as a footballer was with [[Victoria Bammental]] from 1994 until 2000.<ref name="auto1">{{cite web |url=https://www.fr.de/sport/fussball/hansi-flick-bundestrainer-dfb-nationalmannschaft-fussball-trainer-fc-bayern-muenchen-erfolge-90661024.html |title=Hansi Flick: Vom Assistenten zum Triple-Sieger und Bundestrainer |publisher=Frankfurter Rundschau |language=de |date=14 March 2022 |access-date=10 September 2023 |archive-date=11 December 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231211161235/https://www.fr.de/sport/fussball/hansi-flick-bundestrainer-dfb-nationalmannschaft-fussball-trainer-fc-bayern-muenchen-erfolge-90661024.html |url-status=live }}</ref>

Flick never played for the [[Germany national football team]], but made two appearances for the [[Germany national youth football team|Germany under-18 team]], in the [[1983 UEFA European Under-18 Championship#Group A|group stage of the 1983 UEFA European Under-18 Championship]] on 15 and 17 May 1983, in a 1–0 win over [[Sweden men's national football team|Sweden]] and in a 3–1 win over [[Bulgaria national football team|Bulgaria]], respectively.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.dfb.de/en/news/detail/flick-i-have-a-more-relaxed-approach-now-116930/full/1/?no_cache=1&cHash=4905d5a306e866d4c822aafc7ee78ad1 |title=Flick: "I have a more relaxed approach now" |publisher=DFB |date=23 February 2015 |access-date=10 September 2023 |archive-date=9 June 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240609190617/https://www.dfb.de/en/news/detail/flick-i-have-a-more-relaxed-approach-now-116930/full/1/?no_cache=1&cHash=4905d5a306e866d4c822aafc7ee78ad1 |url-status=live }}</ref>

==Managerial career==
Flick's managerial career began in 1996 as a [[Player-coach|player-manager]] of Victoria Bammental, which was playing in the [[Oberliga Baden-Württemberg]] at that time.<ref>{{cite web |last=FC Barcelona |title=Hansi Flick Profile |url=https://www.fcbarcelona.com/en/football/first-team/staff/4030694/hansi-flick |access-date=2026-08-22 |publisher=FCBarcelona.com}}</ref> At the end of the 1997–98 season, the club was relegated to the [[Verbandsliga Baden]], but he remained their coach for two more seasons.<ref name="auto1"/>

===TSG Hoffenheim===
In July 2000, Flick became a manager of the Oberliga Baden-Württemberg side [[TSG 1899 Hoffenheim]], winning the league and gaining promotion to the [[Regionalliga Süd (1994–2012)|Regionalliga Süd]] in his first season at the club. He also won [[North Baden Cup]] four times in a row, between 2002 and 2005. After four unsuccessful attempts to reach the [[2. Bundesliga]], he was released from duties on 19 November 2005.<ref name="DFB">{{cite web |url=https://www.dfb.de/news/detail/flick-als-co-trainer-der-dfb-auswahl-vorgestellt-koepke-verlaengert-bis-2008-8418/ |title=Flick als Co-Trainer der DFB-Auswahl vorgestellt – Köpke verlängert bis 2008 |date=24 August 2006 |website=dfb.de |publisher=[[German Football Association|DFB]] |location=Frankfurt |language=de |trans-title=Flick introduced as assistant coach of the DFB national team – Köpke extends until 2008 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191106210740/https://www.dfb.de/news/detail/flick-als-co-trainer-der-dfb-auswahl-vorgestellt-koepke-verlaengert-bis-2008-8418/ |archive-date=6 November 2019 |url-status=live |access-date=6 November 2019}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.spiegel.de/sport/fussball/retortenclub-dfb-genehmigt-hoffenheimer-fussballfusion-a-387770.html |title=Retortenclub: DFB genehmigt Hoffenheimer Fußballfusion |date=30 November 2005 |newspaper=[[Der Spiegel]] |location=Hamburg |language=de |trans-title=Retort Club: DFB approved Hoffenheim football fusion |access-date=5 November 2019 |archive-date=7 November 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201107040237/https://www.spiegel.de/sport/fussball/retortenclub-dfb-genehmigt-hoffenheimer-fussballfusion-a-387770.html |url-status=live }}</ref> During his tenure at Hoffenheim, Flick graduated first in his class, alongside [[Thomas Doll]], from the [[German Sport University Cologne]], gaining his top coaching credentials in 2003.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://fcbayern.com/en/news/2020/04/contract-until-2023-fc-bayern-and-head-coach-hansi-flick-agree-extension |title=FC Bayern and head coach Hansi Flick agree extension |date=3 April 2020 |website=fcbayern.com |publisher=[[FC Bayern Munich]] |language=en |access-date=20 April 2025}}</ref>

===Red Bull Salzburg (assistant)===
Flick later worked briefly as an assistant of [[Giovanni Trapattoni]] and [[Lothar Matthäus]] and sporting coordinator at [[FC Red Bull Salzburg|Red Bull Salzburg]].<ref name="DFB"/> He stated that his work under Trapattoni, one of the world's most renowned managers, taught him many things, especially on tactics and in developing relations with players, but also said that he disagreed with Trapattoni's defence-first approach.<ref>{{cite magazine |url=http://www.rund-magazin.de/index.php?mact=News,cntnt01,print,0&cntnt01articleid=430&cntnt01showtemplate=false&cntnt01returnid=76 |title=Interview Hansi Flick |website=Rund-Magazin.de |language=de |date=January 2014 |access-date=20 November 2019 |archive-date=7 November 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201107040242/http://www.rund-magazin.de/index.php?mact=News,cntnt01,print,0&cntnt01articleid=430&cntnt01showtemplate=false&cntnt01returnid=76 }}</ref>

===Germany (assistant)===
[[File:Löw-Flick.jpg|thumb|Flick (right) with [[Joachim Löw]] in 2006]]

Flick was named the assistant coach for Germany on 23 August 2006. Although not listed as an officially recognized manager by the [[German Football Association|DFB]], due to the sending off of [[Joachim Löw]] in the previous game, Flick was technically the German manager for the [[UEFA Euro 2008]] quarter final against [[Portugal national football team|Portugal]] on 19 June 2008, which ended in a 3–2 win for Germany. After finishing second at the UEFA Euro 2008 and third at the [[2010 FIFA World Cup]], he reached the semi-finals at the [[UEFA Euro 2012]] and won the [[2014 FIFA World Cup]] as assistant coach of Germany.

=== Germany (sporting director) ===
Flick became [[sporting director]] at the [[German Football Association]] (DFB) after the 2014 World Cup until 16 January 2017.<ref>{{cite web |date=16 January 2017 |title=DFB stimmt Wunsch von Flick nach Vertragsauflösung zu |trans-title=DFB agrees to Flick's request for contract termination |url=https://www.dfb.de/news/detail/dfb-stimmt-wunsch-von-flick-nach-vertragsaufloesung-zu-160600/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201111215653/http://www.dfb.de/news/detail/dfb-stimmt-wunsch-von-flick-nach-vertragsaufloesung-zu-160600/ |archive-date=11 November 2020 |access-date=16 January 2017 |website=dfb.de |publisher=[[German Football Association|DFB]] |language=de |location=Frankfurt}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |date=16 January 2017 |title=Hans-Dieter Flick steps down as German Football Association sporting director |url=http://www.football.co.uk/germany/hansdieter-flick-steps-down-as-german-football-association/8241896/#GoOAcB4kwPI2HR5H.97 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170225133108/http://www.football.co.uk/germany/hansdieter-flick-steps-down-as-german-football-association/8241896/#GoOAcB4kwPI2HR5H.97 |archive-date=25 February 2017 |access-date=7 February 2017 |website=football.co.uk}}</ref>

On 17 April 2021, Flick announced that he had told the club he wanted to leave at the end of the season. He voiced his desire to coach the [[Germany national football team|Germany national team]], given his previous job as an assistant to present German team manager, Joachim Löw. Flick left Bayern with one of the greatest winning records in modern football history. During his tenure, Bayern lost just seven games and won seven out of nine possible trophies ([[Bundesliga]] twice, [[DFB-Pokal]], [[UEFA Champions League]], [[DFL-Supercup]], [[UEFA Super Cup]], [[FIFA Club World Cup]]). Bayern went undefeated in the [[2019–20 UEFA Champions League|2019–20 Champions League]], the first team in European/Champions League history to lift the trophy with a 100 percent win record, and won 23 matches in a row across all competitions between 16 February 2020 and 18 September 2020, a record in German professional football.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.mundodeportivo.com/futbol/fc-barcelona/20240523/1002251836/asi-filosofia-flick-futbol-vertigo-presion-alta-4-2-3-1.html|title=Así es la filosofía Flick: fútbol de vértigo, presión alta y 4-2-3-1|publisher=[[Mundo Deportivo]]|date=23 May 2024|access-date=23 May 2024|language=es|archive-date=23 May 2024|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240523104648/https://www.mundodeportivo.com/futbol/fc-barcelona/20240523/1002251836/asi-filosofia-flick-futbol-vertigo-presion-alta-4-2-3-1.html|url-status=live}}</ref> Flick also coached Bayern to a [[Treble (association football)|treble]], the second treble in Bayern's history. Flick held one of the highest win rates in football history, winning 83% of his games and helped Bayern average 3.0 goals per game across all competitions.<ref name="sportsmax.tv">{{Cite web | title = Flick leaving Bayern: A record win rate and a trophy every 14 games – the key numbers behind departing coach's tenure | url = https://www.sportsmax.tv/index.php/football/international/item/80730-flick-leaving-bayern-a-record-win-rate-and-a-trophy-every-14-games-the-key-numbers-behind-departing-coach-s-tenure | date = 17 May 2021 | access-date = 23 May 2021 | website = sportsmax.tv | archive-date = 23 May 2021 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20210523062140/https://www.sportsmax.tv/index.php/football/international/item/80730-flick-leaving-bayern-a-record-win-rate-and-a-trophy-every-14-games-the-key-numbers-behind-departing-coach-s-tenure | url-status = live }}</ref> In October 2020, Flick won [[UEFA Men's Coach of the Year Award|Europe's Coach of the Year]], an award for the best coach in football in the [[Big Five (association football)|major football leagues of Europe]].<ref name="sportsmax.tv"/>

===Barcelona===
On 29 May 2024, Flick signed as the Manager of [[La Liga]] club [[FC Barcelona|Barcelona]] on a contract until 30 June 2026. He became the third German in the club's coaching history, after [[Hennes Weisweiler]] and [[Udo Lattek]].<ref>{{cite news |date=29 May 2024 |title=Hansi Flick is the new FC Barcelona coach |url=https://www.fcbarcelona.com/en/football/first-team/news/4029878/hansi-flick-is-the-new-fc-barcelona-coach |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240529114441/https://www.fcbarcelona.com/en/football/first-team/news/4029878/hansi-flick-is-the-new-fc-barcelona-coach |archive-date=29 May 2024 |access-date=29 May 2024 |publisher=[[FC Barcelona]]}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Barcelona hires Hansi Flick as coach on a 2-year contract after Xavi's exit. |url=https://apnews.com/article/hansi-flick-barcelona-18608813d4c834f3102c34d23da95065 |access-date=31 May 2024 |website=AP News |date=29 May 2024 |archive-date=31 May 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240531035554/https://apnews.com/article/hansi-flick-barcelona-18608813d4c834f3102c34d23da95065 |url-status=live }}</ref>

Flick won his first league match as Barcelona manager on 17 August 2024, securing a 1–2 comeback victory against [[Valencia CF|Valencia]] at the [[Mestalla Stadium|Mestalla]], breaking Barcelona's two-year streak of 0–0 draws on matchday 1.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Kallas |first=Fernando |date=2024-08-17 |title=Lewandowski double as Barcelona fight back to beat Valencia |url=https://www.reuters.com/sports/soccer/lewandowski-double-barcelona-fight-back-beat-valencia-2024-08-17/ |access-date=2024-08-17 |work=Reuters |language=en}}</ref> He suffered his first La Liga defeat as Barcelona manager in a 4–2 defeat to [[CA Osasuna|Osasuna]] on 28 September.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2024-09-28 |title=Osasuna stuns Barcelona 4-2 and stops it from tying club record for best season start |url=https://apnews.com/article/barcelona-osasuna-liga-fb3a653154605a777148d6149453d1ff |access-date=2024-10-27 |website=AP News |language=en}}</ref> On 23 October, Flick managed a win against his former team Bayern Munich where Barcelona won 4–1 at the [[Estadi Olímpic Lluís Companys]]. Three days later, Flick won his first [[El Clásico]] in charge, a 4–0 domination of [[Real Madrid CF|Real Madrid]] at the [[Santiago Bernabéu Stadium|Bernabéu]].<ref>{{Cite news |last=Kallas |first=Fernando |date=2024-10-26 |title=Barca thrash Real Madrid 4-0 at Bernabeu helped by Lewandowski double |url=https://www.reuters.com/sports/soccer/lewandowski-double-helps-barcelona-stunning-4-0-win-real-madrid-2024-10-26/ |access-date=2024-10-27 |work=Reuters |language=en}}</ref> 

On 12 January 2025, Flick secured his first title with Barcelona by defeating Real Madrid in the [[2025 Supercopa de España|Supercopa de España final]] held at the [[King Abdullah Sports City Stadium]] in [[Jeddah]], [[Saudi Arabia]]. Despite conceding an early goal to [[Kylian Mbappé]], Barcelona responded with a dominant first-half performance, scoring four goals. Even after goalkeeper [[Wojciech Szczęsny]] was sent off, Barcelona maintained control to clinch their 15th Supercopa title with a commanding 5–2 victory.<ref>{{cite web |title=Barcelona thrash Real 5-2 in Spanish Super Cup thriller |url=https://www.reuters.com/sports/soccer/barcelona-thrash-real-5-2-spanish-super-cup-thriller-2025-01-12/ |website=Reuters |date=12 January 2025 |access-date=11 May 2025}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Barcelona thrash Real Madrid in chaotic clásico Spanish Super Cup final |url=https://www.theguardian.com/football/2025/jan/12/barcelona-v-real-madrid-spanish-super-cup-final-match-report |website=The Guardian |date=12 January 2025 |access-date=11 May 2025}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Barcelona 5-2 Real Madrid (Jan 12, 2025) Game Analysis |url=https://www.espn.com/soccer/report/_/gameId/712534 |website=ESPN |date=12 January 2025 |access-date=11 May 2025}}</ref> 

On 26 April 2025, Flick guided Barcelona to a 3–2 extra-time victory over Real Madrid in the [[2025 Copa del Rey final|Copa del Rey final]] at the [[Estadio de La Cartuja]] in Seville, securing the club's 32nd title in the competition.<ref name="theguardian.com">{{cite web |title=Barcelona win thrilling Copa del Rey and drive Madrid to red card fury |url=https://www.theguardian.com/football/2025/apr/27/barcelona-real-madrid-copa-del-rey-match-report |website=The Guardian |date=27 April 2025 |access-date=11 May 2025}}</ref>

On 30 April and 6 May 2025, Flick managed Barcelona in the UEFA Champions League semi-final against [[Inter Milan]]. Despite being eliminated 7–6 on aggregate, his tactical approach received praise for its intensity and attacking intent.<ref>{{cite web |title=Champions League semi-final: Inter hold off Barcelona in 7–6 aggregate thriller |url=https://www.theguardian.com/football/2025/may/07/champions-league-semi-final-inter-barcelona-report |website=The Guardian |date=7 May 2025 |access-date=11 May 2025}}</ref> Flick employed a 4–2–3–1 formation focused on high pressing and wide overloads, with [[Lamine Yamal]] and [[Raphinha]] stretching Inter's defensive shape.<ref>{{cite web |title=How Simone Inzaghi's Inter broke through Flick's high press: tactical analysis |url=https://totalfootballanalysis.com/match-analysis/inter-milan-barcelona-champions-league-tactical-analysis |website=Total Football Analysis |date=9 May 2025 |access-date=11 May 2025}}</ref> While Barcelona created numerous chances and scored six goals over the two legs, their aggressive high line was repeatedly exposed by Inter's direct counter-attacks.<ref>{{cite web |title=Barcelona's brave tactics undone by Inter's counter-punch |url=https://barcauniversal.com/poland-legend-slams-flicks-tactics-after-champions-league-exit-barcelona-screwed-up-everything |website=Barça Universal |date=8 May 2025 |access-date=11 May 2025}}</ref> Despite the elimination, the matches were seen as a reaffirmation of Flick's proactive tactical identity.

Following a 2–0 win over their rivals [[RCD Espanyol|Espanyol]] on 15 May 2025, Flick achieved his first La Liga title with Barcelona, with goals from Lamine Yamal and [[Fermín López]]. This game confirmed Flick's domestic double, as he added his league title to his win in the Copa del Rey.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Kallas |first=Fernando |date=2025-05-15 |title=Barcelona claim 28th LaLiga title after 2–0 win at Espanyol |url=https://www.reuters.com/sports/soccer/barcelona-claim-28th-laliga-title-after-2-0-win-espanyol-2025-05-15/ |access-date=2025-05-16 |work=Reuters |language=en}}</ref>

During the [[2024–25 FC Barcelona season|2024–25 season]], Flick led Barcelona to an unprecedented four victories over Real Madrid in official competitions, including both La Liga fixtures, the Supercopa de España final, and the Copa del Rey final. This marked the first time in the modern era that Barcelona achieved a clean sweep over their rivals in all official matches within a single season.<ref>{{cite web |title=Hansi Flick's unstoppable Barcelona conquer Real Madrid and move to brink of La Liga title |url=https://www.barcablaugranes.com/2025/5/11/24428056/hansi-flicks-unstoppable-barcelona-conquer-real-madrid |website=Barça Blaugranes |date=11 May 2025 |access-date=12 May 2025}}</ref> Then, on 21 May 2025, Flick signed a contract extension until 2027.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2025-05-22 |title=Agreement for Hansi Flick contract extension to 2027 |url=https://www.fcbarcelona.com/en/football/first-team/news/4270507/agreement-for-hansi-flick-contract-extension-to-2027 |access-date=2025-05-22 |website= |publisher=FC Barcelona |language=en}}</ref>

Barcelona continued the good form the following season in completing a double by winning the [[2026 Supercopa de España|Supercopa de España]] in Jeddah, Saudi Arabia, and once again the Spanish league, La Liga, after the [[ Clásico]] in May of the [[2025–26 FC Barcelona season|2025–26 season]] with three games to spare. After that victory, with the side now 14 points ahead, it was mathematically impossible for Real Madrid to win the league.<ref name=liga26>{{cite web|url=https://www.barcablaugranes.com/barcelona-la-liga/119956/ill-never-forget-this-moment-hansi-flick-gets-emotional-after-barcelona-beat-real-madrid-and-seal-la-liga-title| website=barcablaugranes| title='I'll never forget this moment'- Hansi Flick gets emotional after Barcelona beat Real Madrid and seal La Liga title| date=10 May 2026|editor= Gill Clark}}</ref><ref name=super26/> With his success at the club, Flick signed a one year contract extension bringing his time up to 2028.<ref>{{cite web| url=https://www.bbc.com/sport/football/articles/c3v2d7ww36lo| title=Barcelona manager Flick agrees new contract| website=bbc.co.uk| date=12 May 2026}}</ref>

==Manager profile==
While in charge of Bayern Munich, Flick developed the name 'Flicki-Flaka' in the media to characterize the mixed [[gegenpressing]] and possession based style of football the team played.<ref>{{cite web |last=Janssen |first=Sander |url=https://www.vi.nl/pro/flicki-flaka-in-munchen-hoe-hansi-flick-bayern-weer-succesvol-maakte |title=Flicki-Flaka in München: hoe Hansi Flick Bayern weer succesvol maakte |work=Voetbal International |language=nl |date=26 March 2020 |access-date=24 May 2024 |archive-date=24 May 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240524115940/https://www.vi.nl/pro/flicki-flaka-in-munchen-hoe-hansi-flick-bayern-weer-succesvol-maakte |url-status=live }}</ref>

Flick consistently deployed a [[Formation (association football)#4–2–3–1|4–2–3–1]] formation at Bayern Munich featuring a high defensive line that encourages their double pivot and full-backs to adopt positions where they can press the ball as it approaches the midfield third, and to screen and block against switches of play.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.coachesvoice.com/bayern-munich-hansi-flick-bundesliga-coach-watch/ |title=Hansi Flick: Coach Watch |date=25 May 2021 |publisher=The Coaches' Voice |access-date=24 May 2024 |archive-date=24 May 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240524115941/https://www.coachesvoice.com/bayern-munich-hansi-flick-bundesliga-coach-watch/ |url-status=live }}</ref> In Bayern's [[FC Barcelona 2–8 FC Bayern Munich|8–2 win]] over [[FC Barcelona|Barcelona]] en route to winning the [[2019–20 UEFA Champions League]], three out of their first four goals came within ten seconds after regaining possession, as Flick likes to keep passing lanes short with players much closer to the ball. Another tactic that Flick uses to create space on one side is to begin the play on one side of the field and gradually draw the opposition to shift to the ball side.<ref>{{cite web |last=Thyron |first=Stefan |url=https://www.360player.com/blog/the-tactics-that-won-bayern-munich-the-champions-league |title=The Tactics That Won Bayern Munich The Champions League |publisher=360Player |access-date=24 May 2024 |archive-date=24 May 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240524115940/https://www.360player.com/blog/the-tactics-that-won-bayern-munich-the-champions-league |url-status=live }}</ref>

While in charge of the German national team, Flick experimented with fielding a back-three system with indifferent results.<ref>{{cite web |last=Honigstein |first=Raphael |url=https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/4627623/2023/06/21/germany-flick-euros-klopp/ |title=Hansi Flick remains Germany coach only by default as they continue to flounder |work=The New York Times |date=21 June 2023 |access-date=24 May 2024 |archive-date=24 May 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240524120429/https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/4627623/2023/06/21/germany-flick-euros-klopp/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

Upon his appointment as Barcelona's head coach in May 2024, Hansi Flick implemented a tactical system characterized by a high defensive line, coordinated pressing, and an aggressive offside trap.<ref>{{cite web |title=How Hansi Flick's Offside Trap Dominated Real Madrid |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L-b6iJo3osI |website=YouTube |date=2024-10-27 |access-date=2025-05-05}}</ref> This strategy was notably effective in his first El Clásico on 26 October 2024, where Barcelona secured a 4–0 victory over Real Madrid at the  [[Santiago Bernabéu]]. In that match, Real Madrid were caught offside 12 times—the highest number in a La Liga match since 2013—with [[Kylian Mbappé]] alone flagged eight times and having two goals disallowed.<ref>{{cite web |title=Real Madrid Falls into "Offside" Trap Against Barcelona |url=https://www.beinsports.com/en-us/soccer/la-liga/articles-video/real-madrid-falls-into-offside-trap-against-barcelona-2024-10-27 |website=beIN Sports |date=2024-10-27 |access-date=2025-05-05}}</ref>

Flick's Barcelona also exhibited a dynamic attacking approach, blending vertical transitions and short-passing combinations with positional play. The team led La Liga in through balls by mid-season and maintained one of the highest pass completion rates in the league, reflecting Flick's emphasis on close support play and efficient ball circulation.<ref>{{cite web |title=Deconstructing the Flick Method at FC Barcelona |url=https://www.lavanguardia.com/mediterranean/20241030/10063824/deconstructing-flick-method-barcelona-coach-soccer-tactical-analysis-offside-lewandowski-pedri-raphinha.html |website=La Vanguardia |date=2024-10-30 |access-date=2025-05-05}}</ref>

He is also known for his detailed match preparation and strong in-game management. While he traditionally favors a 4–2–3–1 system, Flick has shown flexibility, occasionally employing asymmetrical full-back roles or compact midfield presses depending on the opposition. Under his leadership, players such as Raphinha experienced career-best form, with the Brazilian winger recording 20 goal involvements in the 2024–25 UEFA Champions League group stage alone, breaking [[Lionel Messi]]'s previous club record.<ref>{{cite web |title=Raphinha goes from missing man to Barcelona's Ballon d'Or contender |url=https://www.theguardian.com/football/2025/apr/30/raphinha-barcelona-champions-league-ballon-dor-hansi-flick |website=The Guardian |date=2025-04-30 |access-date=2025-05-05}}</ref>

Although his aggressive offside tactics and high line occasionally leave the team vulnerable to counterattacks, Flick's commitment to a proactive, high-risk style has been credited with restoring both identity and intensity to Barcelona's tactical DNA.<ref>{{cite web |title=Flick, bendito fundamentalista |url=https://as.com/opinion/flick-bendito-fundamentalista-n/ |website=AS |date=2025-05-01 |access-date=2025-05-05}}</ref>

Despite achieving significant success at club level with [[FC Bayern Munich|Bayern Munich]] and [[FC Barcelona|Barcelona]], Flick's tenure with the [[Germany national football team|German national team]] was comparatively underwhelming, marked by early exits and inconsistent performances. Analysts attributed this contrast to the structural differences between club and international football. Flick's preferred high-pressing, vertical and transition-heavy 4–2–3–1 system relies on intense positional drilling and automated coordination, which proved difficult to implement in the limited training windows of international management.<ref>{{cite web |title=Why Hansi Flick's tactics failed with Germany |url=https://theathletic.com/4861002/2023/09/11/germany-hansi-flick-fired-analysis |website=The Athletic |date=11 September 2023 |access-date=12 May 2025}}{{dead link|date=June 2025|bot=medic}}{{cbignore|bot=medic}}</ref>
