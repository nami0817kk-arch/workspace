5 months agoWataru Endo is hugely excited to captain Japan at this summer's World Cup following a challenging campaign on a personal level.
By Glenn Price
PreviousNextThe 33-year-old has recovered from the foot injury he sustained with Liverpool in February and was named in his nation's squad for the tournament in the USA, Mexico and Canada.
He returned to the Reds' matchday 20 this past Sunday for the season-concluder with Brentford at Anfield.
Endo was a guest on the latest episode of FIFPRO's Footballers Unfiltered and was quizzed on a range of topics – read on for the best bits from it.
On the World Cup…
The Japan team, as players, feel like we can do something or we can achieve something or we could surprise the people in the world. The World Cup is always tough to play and every team is a really, really good team and every country are very ready for the World Cup. It's going to be a very tough group stage but we need to play with a confidence.
On being in the same group as the Netherlands and Alexander Isak's Sweden…
Cody Gakpo, before the draw we had a competition, like, 'Maybe we are [in] the same group' or something like that. That happened actually. I spoke with Cody. We have also got Virg [van Dijk], Jeremie Frimpong and Ryan Gravenberch as well, so we have so many Netherlands players at Liverpool at the moment. I'm so excited to play against them.
On his mindset... 
It's a difficult moment for me because I haven't played a lot of games this season and also last season as well. But I think the most important thing is to be ready for every game. Even if you play only five or 10 minutes, you have to play well on the pitch. So, that's what I tried [in] the last few seasons. I feel like I get a little bit older because I'm already 33 years old and I don't play a lot of games, but I still feel like I can play every game for Liverpool or something like that. So, I think to stay positive is also important for me to achieve something.
On his life post-football...
I've started thinking about [life] after my career as well but it doesn't mean I stop playing football next year or something like that. I'm interested in being a manager and I'm also interested in the business of football. I have two of my own books. The latest one is talking about football but also I talk about mindset. Not only as a football player but everyone faces difficult situations in your life, then how you can manage that situation or something like that.
On living in Liverpool... 
I really enjoy living in the UK but it's still difficult to know Scouse actually! I actually don't understand sometimes what they are [saying]. But I really enjoy it. My kids also love living in the UK as well.
On his love for The Beatles growing up... 
Actually, I didn't know The Beatles are from Liverpool. When I was a junior high school student, my English teacher loved The Beatles, so before the class he always played Beatles songs. After that we started the class. Then I moved to Liverpool and then I knew The Beatles are from Liverpool.
On Liverpool supporters…
[For] derbies always the crowd has really strong passion in the stadium. The atmosphere when we score is really crazy at Anfield. I feel like we always have plus one player on the pitch – I think that's why it's so hard to beat Liverpool in Anfield. I think the atmosphere is crazy.
